Modal2D / related research

Shape, strike, sound

Modal2D ↗

Instrument design connects geometry, eigenmodes and excitation. Modal2D explores that relationship through its own procedural instruments, modal tuning and strike maps, with 2D as the main working space.

  • Related research

    Computational Design of Metallophone Contact Sounds ↗

    Gaurav Bharaj, David I. W. Levin, James Tompkin, Yun Fei, Hanspeter Pfister, Wojciech Matusik & Changxi Zheng · SIGGRAPH Asia 2015

    Optimizes geometry to target frequencies and amplitudes, including designs with different notes at specified impact locations. It is a useful research connection for Modal2D’s tuning and strike inspection; Modal2D does not reproduce the paper’s fabrication and geometry-optimization pipeline.

    Paper · multiple strike locations in §9.3 ↗
Inverse Mechanics & Optics / related research

From a desired outcome to a physical construction

Inverse Mechanics & Optics ↗

Motion, caustics and thread images each turn a desired outcome into a constrained construction. These references connect the workbench to established research; its small solvers and target checks have their own scope and limitations.

  • Related research

    Many-Worlds Browsing for Control of Multibody Dynamics ↗

    Christopher D. Twigg & Doug L. James · SIGGRAPH 2007

    Samples physical simulations and lets the user browse and refine passive motions. This is related context for specifying motion outcomes and exploring possible setups; our inverse-design solver uses a different method and is not an implementation of the paper.

  • Related research

    String Art: Towards Computational Fabrication of String Images ↗

    Michael Birsak, Florian Rist, Peter Wonka & Przemyslaw Musialski · EUROGRAPHICS 2018

    Optimizes connected string images and fabricates them robotically. Our workbench uses a greedy continuous chord-density model and an optional weighted fit; its small image-suitability pilot is a diagnostic for those local constraints, not a reproduction of this fabrication pipeline.

  • Related research

    High-contrast Computational Caustic Design ↗

    Yuliy Schwartzburg, Romain Testuz, Andrea Tagliasacchi & Mark Pauly · SIGGRAPH 2014

    A research direction for designing surfaces that form prescribed caustics. It is also useful context for our limits: difficult high-contrast targets in this workbench do not imply a general impossibility in optics. Our sampled smooth height-field solver is a smaller experiment.

Realtime Graphics on CPU ↗RadianceLab ↗WebGPU Atelier ↗

The same visual idea can exercise different execution models: native GLSL post-processing, a C++ image pipeline compiled to WebAssembly, and WGSL post-processing. These adaptations retain the original shader authorship.

    Berger’s brush-stroke and relief-lighting effect is adapted in Realtime Graphics on CPU’s oil-painting mode, WebGPU Atelier’s oil-painting floor and RadianceLab’s GLSL NPR pipeline. The link is the original geometry version cited by the retained shader sources.

  • Shader adaptation

    Ballpoint & ink-spiral treatments ↗

    Florian Berger / flockaroo · source headers dated 2018 and 2016

    RadianceLab’s ballpoint and ink passes retain Berger’s attribution. This link opens the author’s collection; the retained copies do not identify the exact original shader pages.

ShaderWeave / default and recorded examples

A playground for shader compatibility

ShaderWeave ↗

Community shaders exercise the editor, resource graph and multipass compatibility. Their imagery belongs to their original authors; ShaderWeave contributes the host, graph, resource handling and inspection workflow. This selection follows the startup example and named nodes visible in recorded showcases.

  • Compatibility example

    Volumetric Integration ↗

    Sébastien Hillaire / SebH · Frostbite volumetric rendering, SIGGRAPH 2015

    The volumex startup example retains Hillaire’s credit and the Frostbite volumetric-integration explanation. It provides a concrete workload for testing the host’s shader execution and inspection.

    Author’s publication and presentation ↗
  • Compatibility example

    Auroras ↗

    nimitz · 2017

    The Aurora shader is named in the recorded compatibility graph, and its retained header credits nimitz. Its appearance demonstrates a shader running inside the host, rather than an original scene authored for ShaderWeave.

  • Compatibility example

    SmoothLife feedback ↗

    Ricky Reusser · based on Stephan Rafler’s SmoothLife model

    The smoothlife node appears in the feedback showcase. Its shader explicitly credits Reusser’s implementation, connecting a continuous cellular model to the editor’s persistent-buffer workflow.

    Rafler’s SmoothLife paper ↗

The wider library includes additional imported tests; this page credits the identifiable default and recorded examples.

Analytical Render · LTC / method and lookup data

An integral becomes geometry

Analytical Render · LTC ↗

The interactive presentation makes an established area-lighting method inspectable: fit a lobe, transform the emitter, integrate its boundary and compare against a sampled reference.

  • Algorithm reference

    Real-Time Polygonal-Light Shading with Linearly Transformed Cosines ↗

    Eric Heitz, Jonathan Dupuy, Stephen Hill & David Neubelt · SIGGRAPH 2016

    The demo implements the LTC lighting idea and uses GGX lookup data from three.js’s RectAreaLightTexturesLib, derived from Heitz’s reference implementation. The interactive geometry, material studies and comparisons build on that method and data.

    LTC reference code ↗three.js lookup tables ↗

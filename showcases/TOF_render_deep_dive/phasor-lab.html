A sensor measures the sum of path phasors

A direct return and an indirect return can arrive with different delays. Four correlation taps recover their summed modulation phasor; its phase generally describes neither path by itself.

Assumptions and derivation

The scene is monostatic: direct path length is 2z, while the second path length is 2z+ℓextra. With sinusoidal amplitude modulation, four ideal correlation taps at δ=0,π/2,π,3π/2 satisfy Cδ=B+½ Re(P eⁱδ). Their differences recover Re P and −Im P. The common DC offset B cancels.

These phasors describe intensity-modulation delay at MHz frequencies, not the optical electromagnetic carrier. Optical holography uses coherent field interference; CW-ToF uses temporal correlation. The Fourier algebra can be related without identifying the two physical measurements. A near-zero phasor amplitude makes its phase unstable.

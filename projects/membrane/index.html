Minimal Membrane

Surface tension pulls a soap membrane toward lower area; here we simulate that in two ways. Compare direct area minimization with damped physical relaxation on the same constrained membrane. Shared geometry and diagnostics expose how an optimization problem and a dynamical system approach related equilibria.

The ideaA soap film tends to reduce its surface area while remaining attached to its boundary. This project compares moving directly toward lower area with simulating a membrane that moves and gradually loses its motion through damping.

What to watchCompare how the same constrained surface approaches its shape along the two routes.

The idea behind it

Could two very different update rules arrive at the same shape? A shared membrane makes that question tangible: direct minimization and damped motion can approach related equilibria while revealing very different routes, oscillations and stability limits.

Working intuition Related final shapes can conceal very different update rules, trajectories and stability behavior.

Open the application, then follow what changes

Two routes toward a constrained membrane: step directly downhill in area, or integrate the same area force through damped motion. Shared geometry and diagnostics reveal how optimization and physical relaxation approach related equilibria, and how added load changes the target.

Compare the model, not just the final picture

A fixed boundary constrains a triangular disk mesh. Both branches compute triangle-area gradients: the mint optimizer steps directly downhill, while the amber physical branch integrates the same force through retained, damped velocity. Optional downward load changes the physical branch’s target.

Choose overlay or side-by-side presentation independently of the difference arrows. The diagnostic compares corresponding mesh vertices. Its signed-height colors and arrows reveal transient and settled disagreement; they do not measure nearest-surface distance or prove that two surfaces intersect.

Boundary design drives the surface

Smooth boundary presets include coils, orbits, cages, and knot-like loops. Changing the boundary reshapes the problem while keeping the solver comparison intact.

Both branches act on the triangulated surface and include tangential smoothing. At zero external load their area forces have the same stationarity condition, but inertia, damping, mesh discretization and initialization can lead to different trajectories or equilibria. The display does not certify a unique global minimum.

Make the surface legible

An interference shader gives the membrane an iridescent appearance with animated thickness variation. Lighting and surface color help reveal shape without changing the underlying comparison.

Try a cage boundary in overlay mode, let both solvers settle, and then add gravity to the physical model. The divergence illustrates the role of the objective and external forces.

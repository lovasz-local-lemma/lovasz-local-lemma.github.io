<header><nav><a href="../bdpt-audit/index.html">Transport coverage &amp; BDPT audit</a><a href="../volume-transport/index.html">Volume transport &amp; material boundaries</a><a href="validation/20260913/index.html">Recorded native comparisons</a></nav><h1>What guides the raster image?</h1><p>Full-resolution geometry supplies the immediate image; sampled transport supplies appearance that the raster shading does not reproduce. The old methods reuse a tracer picture. Enhanced separates linear surface appearance, extinction and photon scattering before display mapping.</p><p><strong>“Guided” here means image compositing, not path-guided importance sampling.</strong> These options do not learn a distribution for choosing future path directions.</p></header>
<section><h2>Three routes, different contracts</h2><div class="scroll"><table><thead><tr><th>Method</th><th>What is computed?</th><th>How it reaches the image</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td>Old Screen-Space Blend</td><td>The shared GPU tracer runs at viewport dimensions multiplied by the general resolution scale; lower resolution is optional, not intrinsic.</td><td>Each raster fragment samples the tracer's already display-mapped image at the matching screen coordinate. Facing and albedo modifications are applied, then a fixed 82% guide blend mixes it with raster shading.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Old “Vertex-Baked”</td><td>The same tracer image. No persistent vertex-color or UV lighting bake is created by this mode.</td><td>The fragment's world position is projected through the current camera matrix to sample that image. It uses a different facing/albedo treatment and an 88% blend. Since the projection is the current camera, coordinates largely coincide with screen-space lookup; moving the camera does not reveal a reusable, view-independent lighting bake.</td></tr>
<tr><td>Enhanced surface guide</td><td>A dedicated progressive GPU path tracer uses MIS direct lighting and surface bounces, with medium interactions disabled. It has its own accumulation and resolution control.</td><td>A 3×3 reconstruction admits samples only when guide-hit distance matches full-resolution raster receiver depth. It blends linear accumulation with raster shading, then composites medium and maps the result for display once.</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div><p>The old modes are useful artistic previews, but a display-mapped tracer color is not an irradiance map: multiplying it by albedo/facing again is an appearance adjustment. The old branch also reuses the shared tracer state; it is not a separate, tightly specified surface-only estimator. Enhanced disables that old guided branch while active.</p></section>
<section><h2>Enhanced: surfaces → transmission → scattering</h2><ol><li><strong>Raster geometry and materials:</strong> draw at full display resolution. Preserve raster shading where the guide has no matching receiver.</li><li><strong>Vacuum surface guide:</strong> progressively trace surface appearance. Its default scale is 0.5 in each dimension, subject to a 786,432-pixel budget with aspect ratio preserved.</li><li><strong>Photon scattering film:</strong> run the selected photon-primitive family in a separate integrator. This CPU film is capped at 65,536 pixels and uploaded as linear RGB. It does not add a second copy of surface shading.</li><li><strong>Composite:</strong> compute camera-to-receiver optical depth through supported homogeneous boxes/spheres. Apply transmission to the surface layer; add the already attenuated scattering film; apply exposure, tone mapping and gamma once.</li></ol><p class="formula">C = exp(−τ) · mix(raster surface, accepted guide, blend) + scattering</p><p>At guide blend 1, accepted pixels use the traced surface estimate; full-resolution raster visibility still governs admission. This cannot recover fine shading detail absent from the low-resolution guide. The medium film has a separate depth-aware screen filter.</p><p>Camera, scene and transport-setting changes invalidate accumulation. There is no motion-reprojected temporal history in this compositor. Display blend, exposure and screen blur can change without invalidating the transport film.</p></section>
<section><h2>Photon execution is another independent choice</h2><ul><li><strong>Independent:</strong> sample primitives independently for camera samples and accumulate over passes.</li><li><strong>Shared batch:</strong> gather the same primitive batch across the image. Normalize by attempted draws, not by successful intersections.</li><li><strong>Raster where implemented:</strong> use conservative raster gathering for supported records, including UV plane, beam and analytical hourglass. Unsupported families fall back to shared gathering; the status reports the actual execution.</li><li><strong>Fast hourglass / mesh sheets:</strong> triangulate finite refracted sheets and rasterize their contribution. Angular resolution, filtering and grazing regularization make this a biased structure preview. Here it supplies refracted caustics; its ordinary-fog and surface companion layers are disabled.</li></ul><p>A medium-method MIS choice combines that family's supported strategies. It does not mean that the surface guide and raster image are MIS-weighted, or that every family has a raster implementation. A selected caustic-only method can leave ordinary direct fog absent.</p></section>
<section><h2>What remains approximate?</h2><p>This is a pinhole appearance preview. The vacuum surface guide plus camera-leg extinction does not reproduce extinction on secondary reflected/refracted surface paths, complete surface–medium exchange, depth of field, or a separate directly visible volume-emission layer.</p><p>The current GPU surface guide lacks arbitrary triangle-mesh intersections. Depth rejection prevents painting a traced background onto an unmatched foreground mesh, but cannot supply missing mesh shadows, reflections or indirect transport. The mesh-sheet caustic path has its own triangle intersections.</p><p>Heterogeneous, simulated, procedural and density-node media retain the old raster volume preview, with the vacuum guide disabled. Overlapping homogeneous optical depths are added in the composite; this can differ from the native medium-selection convention. More samples reduce noise but do not remove compositing and filtering bias.</p></section>

<section><h2>Environment LT: coverage does not imply efficiency</h2><p>The new sampler makes environment light available to pure LT. It samples a uniform sky direction and a uniform entry disk around the scene, then traces inward. This is a real source sampler, not just a background painted behind geometry.</p><p>Many emitted rays miss useful geometry, hit occluded regions, or produce little contribution to the current camera. A loose scene bound wastes disk samples, while uniform directions spend samples on dim parts of a gradient sky. Eye-side sampling starts at a visible receiver, so it can spend much more of its effort on the image's relevant transport.</p><p>Thus ordinary broad environment illumination is often a poor use of this LT sampler. That is not a universal failure of environment light tracing: concentrated specular transport can favor light-side paths. Directional importance sampling and tighter bounds can improve efficiency; environment emission alone is not either of those optimizations.</p><p>Our radius test preserved mean brightness while larger-radius runs had higher receiver RMSE, consistent with wasted launch area. It was a normalization regression, not an equal-time benchmark against an optimized environment sampler. See <a href="../bdpt-audit/index.html#environment-lt">the measured results and limitations</a>.</p></section>

<p class="eyebrow">Implementation study · volume transport · 15 September 2026</p><h1>One interior.<br>Several ways to transport its light.</h1>
<p class="lead">A medium changes what happens along a ray. A dielectric boundary changes which ray enters it. An estimator decides how to sample the resulting paths. Keeping these three responsibilities separate makes it possible to compare volume path tracing, cached photon mapping and connection/merging methods on the same physical scene.</p>
<div class="pipeline"><div class="step" style="--accent:var(--gold)"><b>η</b><strong>Cross the boundary</strong><p>Passthrough preserves direction; glass reflects and refracts.</p></div><div class="step" style="--accent:var(--mint)"><b>σ</b><strong>Travel inside</strong><p>Scattering, absorption and a phase function define the interior.</p></div><div class="step" style="--accent:var(--blue)"><b>∫</b><strong>Sample the transport</strong><p>Follow collisions, connect vertices or gather light deposits.</p></div><div class="step" style="--accent:var(--violet)"><b>ρ</b><strong>Reconstruct the image</strong><p>Path averages and finite-radius photon density have different error behavior.</p></div></div></header>
<section id="shaped-shadows"><p class="eyebrow">Correctness repair · bounded medium shadows</p><h2>A volume boundary is not a solid fog ball.</h2><p>The old shadow-connection helper multiplied by exp(−σₜ × distance inside the bounding shape). It never evaluated the actual SDF, noise or reaction-diffusion density. Camera paths did evaluate that density, so naive tracing could show a hollow or patterned shadow while NEE and camera-completed LT/BDPT produced a filled-in silhouette.</p><p>Shadow connections now evaluate the same base density patterns, using ratio tracking through the bounded segment. Homogeneous media retain the exact Beer–Lambert shortcut. This applies to area-light and point-light connections; the defect was not a restriction of NEE to one light type.</p><p class="formula">T = exp(−∫ σₜρ(x) dx), &nbsp; T̂ = ∏ⱼ [1 − ρ(xⱼ)]</p><p>Here the candidate positions form a Poisson process at rate σₜ, and the supported base density is bounded between zero and one. Empty holes contribute a factor of one. A finite number of candidates adds sampling noise, but does not turn the enclosing sphere or box into homogeneous extinction. The implementation does not impose a fixed marching-step count.</p><p class="note">This repair covers the base procedural density models. Independently overridden scattering/absorption coefficients from advanced node graphs or decoupled slots still need their own matching extinction/majorant treatment; these are not certified by the base-density tests.</p></section>
<section id="upbp-beam-records"><p class="eyebrow">UPBP · inspect light transport</p><h2>Bounded-medium photon beams, without adding global fog</h2><p>Enable <strong>UPBP → Enable Medium</strong>, then <strong>Visualization → Record + Show Photon Beams</strong>. Changing the visualization requests a fresh light-path cache. The global-fog option is independent: it is not required for recording segments inside a passthrough volume box or a volume sphere behind a dielectric shell.</p><p>The old controls coupled segment storage to global fog and did not rebuild when beam visualization was enabled. A collision-only cache could therefore remain empty. Beam recording now explicitly requests light paths even when point merging is off, and the overlay searches beyond the first 500 emitted paths. The displayed beam count refers to stored segments; only a capped subset is drawn on screen.</p><p>These are <strong>diagnostic beam records</strong>. They show the sampled free-flight segments, clipped to medium boundaries, with incoming segment power before the terminal collision’s absorption/scattering weight. They do not introduce a beam-gathering or beam–beam MIS estimator. The implemented medium renderer remains collision VPT and volume-point gathering. A finite area or point emitter must illuminate the volume; the current UPBP light-path builder does not launch photons from an environment-only scene.</p><details><summary>Bounded-medium regression</summary><p>With global fog and point merging disabled, 12,000 launched paths produced 1,894 recorded segments in a passthrough cube, 803 in a passthrough sphere, and 1,483 in a sphere inside a separate glass shell. Every endpoint was checked against the medium boundary; counts matched the overlay’s path records. Turning recording off restored the collision-only cache shortcut. Existing Beer/Fresnel, scattering, photon-gathering and point-source-through-glass tests also passed.</p></details></section>
<section id="transport-completion"><p class="eyebrow">Coverage · complementary estimators</p><h2>Fill missing transport, and show who supplied it</h2>
<p>Under <strong>Render Settings → Light Strategy</strong>, enable <strong>Fill missing transport with backup estimator</strong>. For light tracing, choose <strong>Naive PT (BSDF only)</strong> or <strong>NEE camera PT</strong>. The display can keep original colors, show the backup contribution in red, or isolate the backup. The toggle defaults off so pure estimator limitations remain visible. Pyramid display filters are paused while completion is enabled; the completion views provide the attribution instead.</p>
<p>This is a partition of contributions, not a repair of black pixels. A dark pixel may be physically correct or merely unconverged. Instead, camera paths contribute only when the implemented light-side strategy has no support for that path class. On the supported partition, the native estimator retains sole ownership. If χ identifies the supported subset, the construction is L = ∫χF + ∫(1−χ)F. The two domains do not overlap; no averaging of two full renders is involved.</p>

<p>The red display is a diagnostic decomposition, not physical red illumination. Native contributions keep their color and only the backup is shown as red luminance, so mixed pixels can contain both. Splats, clamping and existing approximate BSDF implementations retain their previous limitations. The partition targets the renderer’s known camera/source support; it is not a proof of completeness for every advanced material, projector, temporal filter or medium configuration.</p>
<p>The theoretical foundation is complementary path sampling and support-aware weighting, as described in <a href="https://www.pbr-book.org/3ed-2018/Light_Transport_III_Bidirectional_Methods/Bidirectional_Path_Tracing">PBRT’s bidirectional path tracing chapter</a>. The useful addition here is an explicit coverage control and a visible attribution of the filled transport, rather than a claim to have invented estimator combination.</p>
<details><summary>Validation and interpretation</summary><p>The production GPU shader’s mirror scene gives a total-radiance ratio of 1.006 against the camera NEE reference without medium, and 1.035 with one homogeneous medium. These finite-sample comparisons do not establish a universal error bound. Tests also isolate the backup, verify that red coding changes only the backup channels, check perfect-pinhole replacement, reject duplicate camera contributions for finite-aperture naive LT, and exercise missing point/environment sources. These are coverage checks, not a universal performance or unbiasedness certification.</p></details></section>
<section id="strict-estimators"><p class="eyebrow">Estimator identity · deliberate limitations</p><h2>What is actually rendering this image?</h2><p><strong>Allow VPT completion fallback</strong>, under Light Tracer Parameters, makes the existing fallback explicit. It defaults on to preserve the usable preview. If the active medium cannot be handled by the selected LT/BDPT kernel, the enabled fallback runs camera-side MIS/VPT and labels that fact. Disable it to obtain intentionally black unsupported output. It does not enable an unimplemented heterogeneous BDPT algorithm.</p><div class="scroll"><table><thead><tr><th>Selected method</th><th>Perfect pinhole</th><th>Specular transport</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Naive LT</td><td>Random rays have zero probability of hitting the ideal point aperture. Pure output is black.</td><td>Can follow specular bounces and physically hit a finite aperture; that differs from connecting an arbitrary specular vertex to a point camera.</td></tr><tr><td>Camera-connection LT</td><td>Explicitly connects to the camera point, so diffuse receivers remain visible.</td><td>Can follow glass/mirror bounces before a diffuse receiver. An arbitrary terminal ideal-specular vertex cannot be connected to the camera.</td></tr><tr><td>VPT completion fallback</td><td>Camera paths work normally.</td><td>Mirror/glass appearance can come from camera paths. It is not evidence that pure LT acquired this coverage.</td></tr></tbody></table></div><p>With transport completion off, pure LT discards all camera-side radiance, including volume and environment contributions that could previously leak through while surface-emitter contributions were suppressed. The UI no longer describes pure LT as “Eye: MIS + Light”. The finite-pinhole warning applies only to naive LT.</p><details><summary>Verification</summary><p>GPU regression on the RTX 4080 Laptop: eight production shadow-integration cases (vacuum, homogeneous, hollow, varying density, medium disabled and clipped segments) match independent Beer integrals within 0.009 absolute transmittance. Production rendering kernels additionally verify zero naive-LT energy at a perfect pinhole, nonzero camera-connection LT energy, zero strict unsupported output and nonzero explicit VPT completion. Both the full heterogeneous kernel and compact homogeneous BDPT kernel compile.</p></details><p>A true zero-area point light also differs from a small emissive sphere named “Point Emitter”. Naive camera paths cannot randomly hit a mathematical point emitter, but can hit a finite sphere or the ceiling light. A shadow visible in naive mode therefore does not by itself identify which light produced it. Compare one enabled source at a time.</p><p>For the camera-connection distinction, see <a href="https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Cameras_and_Film/Camera_Interface">PBRT’s camera importance-sampling interface</a>.</p></section>


<section id="reading-the-view"><h2>Read the active renderer before interpreting the tiles</h2><p><strong>VPT fallback</strong> means the current medium is outside the compact BDPT kernel’s support. It is not a collection of equivalent BDPT strategies. The strategy pyramid is paused in that state and the full VPT image is shown instead. A noisy or emissive medium can select this route even when the menu still requests BDPT.</p><p>For a supported medium, the pyramid groups paths by eye/light vertex counts. Each real volume scattering event adds a vertex; null tracking events are not extra physical scattering vertices. Several strategy images can resemble one another because they estimate the same path-length contribution with different sampling constructions. The <strong>Max displayed edges</strong> slider limits the visible length range without changing the transport budget or MIS weights.</p><p><strong>PM:</strong> enable “VPM: cached volume photons” and inspect its cache-status line. The old “No Vol” badge was stale. The current VPM cache requires homogeneous bounded media; it does not render procedural or emissive interiors. Its active status reports actual cached collision photons. Scenes 115 and 116 are controlled examples.</p><p><strong>UPBP:</strong> CPU execution is only one cost. Each pass traces paths, rebuilds photon neighborhoods and queries them along camera paths. The standalone route no longer builds a duplicate auxiliary map; collision-only mode skips light-map work unless diagnostic light paths are requested. Point reconstruction can still be expensive and noisy, and the CPU heterogeneous density model does not reproduce every GPU volume type. These are distinct from estimator coverage. Beam estimators may reduce variance for selected transport; simply adding beams does not establish correctness or guarantee speed.</p></section>

<section id="fixtures"><p class="eyebrow">A matched native experiment</p><h2>Keep the interior. Change the shell.</h2><p>The new <strong>Volume boundary</strong> scene presets contain one neutral scattering sphere, a concentric surface shell, two colored Lambertian area lights and diffuse calibration blocks outside the medium. Each renderer gets the same geometry, source power, camera and exposure. The two shell variants change only the boundary material and its IOR.</p>
<div class="scroll"><table><thead><tr><th>Renderer</th><th>Direction-preserving shell</th><th>Dielectric shell · IOR 1.5</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>VPT</td><td>Scene 113</td><td>Scene 114</td></tr><tr><td>Cached VPM</td><td>Scene 115</td><td>Scene 116</td></tr><tr><td>UPBP</td><td>Scene 117</td><td>Scene 118</td></tr></tbody></table></div><p>The fixtures reset display filters, photon selection and progressive-radius settings. PT, PM and UPBP use a 12-bounce budget; selecting compact BDPT imposes its smaller, shared eight-event maximum. Match that lower cap explicitly when comparing BDPT.</p>
<p>The homogeneous interior has radius 1.38, σ<sub>s</sub> = 1.2, σ<sub>a</sub> = 0.08 and HG g = 0.35. The smooth shell has radius 1.4. Its small separation from the medium boundary avoids coincident-hit ambiguity. It represents one dielectric interface enclosing a scattering interior; it is not a hollow glass bottle with an additional inner glass–liquid interface.</p>
<p>There is no world fog, environment illumination, hidden point source or volume emission. If the space around the spheres fills with mist, that is an accounting error. The medium remains active after a camera or photon ray refracts through the shell; “currently inside glass” must not suppress a separately declared overlapping volume.</p>
<details><summary>How to make the comparison informative</summary><ol><li>Load a paired scene and keep its authored settings. For BDPT, start from 113 or 114, keep Volume Medium enabled, and choose BDPT with Balance or Power on a perfect pinhole. The legacy participating-media showcase switch is not required for the compact kernel.</li><li>Compare both equal elapsed time and converged linear radiance. Equal sample count alone hides the cost of connections, cached photons and gathers.</li><li>Inspect the interior separately from the calibration blocks. Directly illuminated diffuse blocks test normalization; the sphere tests scattering and boundary crossing.</li><li>For VPM, increase photon count while reducing the kernel radius. A blurry image is not necessarily darker transport; finite-radius leakage and missing transport are different failures.</li><li>Test zero scattering, then pure absorption, then scattering. At zero extinction the passthrough sphere should vanish; glass should remain refractive.</li><li>Record path-depth limits. A dense medium can require many scattering events; a short hard cap is a truncated transport model even when its individual weights are correct.</li></ol></details></section>

<section id="sss"><p class="eyebrow">Material model versus transport phenomenon</p><h2>Is subsurface scattering different from glass with medium inside?</h2><p><strong>Subsurface scattering is the phenomenon:</strong> light enters an object, scatters internally and leaves, often at another position. An explicit dielectric boundary plus a participating interior can model it directly. Glass without internal scattering is a different case: it bends light and may absorb it, but does not generate volume scattering by itself.</p>
<div class="columns"><div><h3>Explicit interior paths</h3><p>Trace Fresnel reflection/refraction at entry and exit, sample distances to interior collisions, apply the phase function and account for absorption. This can retain thin-region transmission, low-order scattering, internal reflections and detailed geometry. Optically thick objects may need long paths.</p></div><div><h3>Diffusion / BSSRDF shortcut</h3><p>A BSSRDF describes transport between surface entry and exit points without explicitly constructing every interior event. A diffusion model is most appropriate after many scattering events; it is less reliable for thin layers, small optical thickness and strongly directional transport.</p></div></div>
<p>The classic <a href="https://graphics.stanford.edu/~srm/publications/SG01-subsurf-abstract.html">Jensen–Marschner–Levoy–Hanrahan model</a> combines a single-scattering term with a dipole diffusion approximation for multiple scattering. Its approximation is a different estimator/model choice, not the definition of SSS.</p>
<details open><summary>What RadianceLab’s existing “Dipole SSS” material actually does</summary><p>The GPU material named <strong>Dipole SSS</strong> uses a simplified sphere-only diffusion-style hop. It samples shell reflection, forms reduced scattering σ′<sub>s</sub> = (1 − g)σ<sub>s</sub> and a diffusion scale, samples an exponential lateral travel distance, rotates that distance onto the sphere, then samples a cosine-weighted outgoing direction. A tint and approximate Fresnel/diffusion factor scale the result.</p><p>This is <strong>not the complete published dipole BSSRDF</strong>: it does not evaluate the real/virtual-source dipole profile and does not trace an interior random walk. Its sphere lookup also depends on the material index, so one material should belong to one sphere. The explicit medium-in-glass fixtures use a Glass material and a separate VolumeSphere instead. They do not invoke that shortcut.</p><p>The raster screen-space SSS blur is a third, appearance-oriented approximation. Neither its attractive glow nor the diffusion material’s label establishes agreement with the explicitly transported interior.</p></details></section>

<section id="estimators"><h2>Three estimators, three useful views of the same integral</h2><div class="columns"><div><h3>VPT follows collisions</h3><p>Each ray chooses whether and where a collision occurs. Survival, scattering density, phase sampling and source sampling have to match their contribution weights. Sampling from a free-flight exponential already accounts for part of transmission; multiplying by the same attenuation again darkens the estimate.</p></div><div><h3>VPM reconstructs from deposits</h3><p>Light paths leave volume photon records. The camera queries a three-dimensional neighborhood, applies the incoming-direction phase factor and reconstructs in-scattered light. A volume kernel normalizes in units of 1/volume, not the 1/area used by a surface photon map.</p></div></div><h3>Coverage comes before combination</h3><p>A point emitter behind a specular boundary is a useful warning. Collision VPT with ordinary next-event connections cannot produce the required bent delta-source connection, while emitted photons can refract through the shell and then scatter. Averaging those estimates would halve that missing component. When true point sources and specular geometry coexist, this prototype therefore selects photon gathering for finite-source transport if it is enabled. Collision VPT alone remains incomplete for those paths. This conservative rule is not a replacement for a complete path-space MIS implementation.</p><h3>UPBP combines complementary sampling constructions</h3><p>The published <a href="https://www.iliyan.com/publications/PointsBeamsPaths/">Unifying Points, Beams, and Paths</a> method combines volumetric density estimates and path sampling with a common weighting framework. The benefit comes from complementary sampling behavior: one representation can find transport that another estimates with high variance. Our current prototype’s implemented subset is stated separately; a name or a beam visualization is not evidence that the entire published algorithm is present.</p><p>Likewise, PP’s photon-point strategy and cached VPM are related density-estimation ideas with different execution contracts. The latter deliberately retains a light-side cache for reuse across camera queries. Sharing an underlying kernel idea does not make their cache lifetime, cost or sampling distribution identical.</p></section>

<section id="validation"><h2>Validation record</h2><p>The scene catalogue regression passes <strong>835,669</strong> geometry, source, medium and paired-mode checks, including all six new fixtures. It checks identical geometry and illumination across estimators, a strictly enclosed homogeneous interior, matching CPU/GPU area-light records and the authored camera settings.</p><h3>GPU BDPT · homogeneous HG regression</h3><p>A small analytical scene contains one bounded homogeneous medium with σ<sub>s</sub> = 0.35, σ<sub>a</sub> = 0.07, g = 0.3 and radius 3, plus diffuse receivers and a finite emitter. The common depth cap is two.

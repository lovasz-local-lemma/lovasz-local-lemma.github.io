The methods differ first in how they construct the refracted family, then in how the camera evaluates it.</p><div class="flow"><article class="card"><b>Strategy 20</b><h2>Derive</h2><p>An analytical sphere turns the swept refracted family into an axis-revolved hourglass. Camera rays solve crossings against that geometry and use its Jacobian.</p></article><article class="card"><b>Strategy 32</b><h2>Reconstruct</h2><p>Finite rays travel through triangle glass. Compatible neighboring histories become triangular sheets; camera rays intersect a sheet BVH and gather the crossings.</p></article><article class="card"><b>Fast preview</b><h2>Project</h2><p>Either family can be projected into a reduced-resolution image. Triangles are rasterized, filtered and enlarged instead of gathered independently by each camera ray.</p></article></div><section><h2>Where the approximations enter</h2><table><thead><tr><th>Method</th><th>What it gains</th><th>What remains approximate</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Analytical 20</td><td>Exact continuous one-sphere sheet; sharp singular structure</td><td>Restricted geometry and path support; optional grazing guard can discard energy</td></tr><tr><td>Normal 32</td><td>Concavity, holes, varying mesh normals and chains of glass objects</td><td>Finite angular sampling, adaptive topology cuts and a grazing cutoff; more passes do not remove fixed tessellation error</td></tr><tr><td>Fast preview</td><td>Responsive viewfinder and fractional pixel coverage of narrow sheets</td><td>Screen resolution, interpolation, tangency clamp, blur, occlusion-edge leakage, and omitted camera-side specular sheet views</td></tr></tbody></table></section><section><h2>Why 32 handles harder geometry</h2><p>It is the numerical light-side construction that matters. Strategy 20 knows the closed-form geometry of a sphere. Strategy 32 instead asks the scene where each refracted ray goes, so the same construction can follow a monkey, teapot, torus or several separate glass bodies. Its finite angular tessellation makes it approximate, but bias is not the source of its geometric reach.</p><p class="verdict">20 derives the sheet. 32 reconstructs it numerically. Fast preview rasterizes either one.</p><p>The preview can look better sooner on thin folds and near singularities because fractional pixel coverage does not wait for a camera sample to land on a narrow feature, while its grazing floor and filter suppress extreme values. That is a useful visualization trade: it changes the limiting image. For measurements, use normal 32 and increase both pass count and angular resolution. For framing a caustic or inspecting a chain, enable <code>Fast structure preview (biased)</code>.</p></section></main></html>

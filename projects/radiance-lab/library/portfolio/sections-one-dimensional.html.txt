      <p class="sec-label">04b — The lattice's other half</p>
      <h2>Sweep <i>one</i> coordinate instead of two</h2>
      <p class="lead">A photon leaves a vertex with a direction and a distance: three numbers, <b>(θ, φ, t)</b>, in a frame at that vertex. Fix one and sweep two and you get a surface — the cone, the disk, the sphere, kernel-free and shipped since the first week. Fix <em>two</em> and sweep one and you get a curve. There are exactly three of those, and until this week only one of them was built.</p>
      <p><b>The construction is the user's.</b> The ring and the arc, the shared measure that mixes them with the beam, and the observation that closes the lattice are all theirs; what was added here is the transcription into the renderer and the gates below.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="narrow">
      <h3>One expression, not three constructions</h3>
      <p>Every member of the bottom row is the same integrand times the same kernel <span class="mono">1/(2r)</span>, divided by one geometric term: <span class="mono">|(∂x/∂u) × d|</span>, where <span class="mono">u</span> is whichever coordinate is being swept and <span class="mono">d</span> is the camera direction. <b>The tracer beam's familiar 1/sin θ is that expression for a unit-speed sweep.</b> The beam was written first and looked like a special case; it is the <span class="mono">t</span> column of a lattice that now closes. The <b>ring</b> (strategy 28) sweeps φ, so its locus is a circle of radius <span class="mono">t sin θ</span> about the frame axis; the <b>arc</b> (29) sweeps θ, so its locus is a half-circle of radius <span class="mono">t</span> about the vertex itself. Write the volume element as <span class="mono">t² dt dμ dφ</span> and the point source's <span class="mono">1/t²</span> cancels it, which is why the ring carries one factor of <span class="mono">1/t</span> that the beam does not, and the arc carries that plus an explicit sine.</p>

      <h3>Ray versus circle is a quartic — and the primitive is a torus</h3>
      <p>Intersecting a camera ray with a circle of tube radius <span class="mono">r</span> is ray-versus-torus, which is what these primitives actually are, and the stationary-point condition squares to a quartic. Two things make the shipped solver simpler than the textbook one. The bracket is free and exact — every stationary point lies in <span class="mono">[−k−ρ, −k+ρ]</span> — and inside that bracket the quartic's own derivative is a cubic in closed form, whose real roots cut the interval into at most four monotone pieces, each sign change bisected. No companion matrix, no eigenvalues, <b>and no threshold to guess</b>.</p>
      <p>The part that is easy to get wrong is that you must <b>sum every stationary point</b>, minima and saddles alike. Two or three roots occur on <b>57.01 %</b> of hits when the camera ray runs near coplanar with the ring's circle — 51.15 % two-root and 5.86 % three-root over 165,429 measured hits — and those are precisely the configurations the primitive exists for. Keeping only the nearest root would have deleted the geometry the estimator was built to reach, and would have looked like a mild darkening rather than a bug.</p>

      <h3>The consequence that matters: the swept density stops cancelling</h3>
      <p>In the two-dimensional members the sampling density of the swept coordinate cancels against the emission profile, so the profile is divided out and never evaluated. Sweep one coordinate and it does not cancel, and that single fact sets the whole character of the group. The emission profile has to be <b>evaluated at the crossing</b> — which is why any of these is identically zero at a smooth dielectric (a two-dimensional delta integrated over one dimension is still a delta), and equally why an evaluated payload is exactly what would let a one-dimensional member carry a bounce off a <em>rough</em> surface. A delta in one angle is fine, and the family already ships it: the cone is the polar-delta ring.</p>
      <p class="note"><b>A corollary that was run as written on 2026-09-08, and half of it fell.</b> If the emission profile is axisymmetric about the frame axis, the ring's swept coordinate <em>is</em> the symmetry coordinate, and a μ-density shaped like the profile absorbs it entirely — <span class="mono">f_e / q</span> is a constant and the profile is never evaluated. That is why the frame axis is the light normal: a Lambertian emitter is axisymmetric about it by construction. Until 2026-09-08 this note cited a <span class="mono">cos⁴</span> lobe reading 1.0087 ± 0.0184 as the verification; that run sampled μ uniformly and <em>evaluated</em> the profile at the crossing — the opposite experiment. Run with the absorbing sampler (<span class="mono">run_lobe_absorb.py</span>, μ ~ 5μ⁴, same seed and N) the ring reads 0.987 ± 0.017, 1.018 ± 0.008 and 0.983 ± 0.011 on the broadside, steep and along-axis cameras — unbiased, like the evaluated one — with 1.2×, 2.6× and 2.5× <em>less</em> variance. On the near-tangent camera it has <b>20× more</b>: the absorbing density rarely visits the μ ≈ 0 rings the profile is dark on and pays them full weight, which is exactly where the ring's tangency family lives. The corollary is right about the profile and wrong about "pays nothing": where the singular family sits in the profile's dark, evaluating is the cheaper deal, and the shipped ring evaluates.</p>

      <h3>Three tangency families, and none of them overlap</h3>
      <p>The ring goes singular where the camera ray is coplanar with its circle. The arc goes singular where the ray runs along the frame axis. <b>The beam goes singular nowhere</b> — its parallelism needs two <em>fixed</em> directions to line up by coincidence, while a swept circle hunts the tangency. That disjointness is the argument for mixing them, and it is visible one derivation earlier than any film: at 800,000 samples per cell against a quadrature reference the ring alone reads <b>2.1323 ± 0.0516</b> on a near-coplanar camera and unity everywhere else — an excess of 1.13 against a bar of 0.05, <b>22 sigma, resolved</b>. <b>Only half of that pair is a measurement, and the half that is not is marked.</b> The arc's along-axis cell reads 1.0286 ± 0.0269, an excess of 0.0286 against a bar of 0.0269 — <b>1.1 sigma, not resolved</b> — and it was bolded here as a measured family on the strength of its centre alone. The arc's tangency family is <em>indicated</em> by this table and <em>demonstrated</em> by its worst-pixel column below, 41.7 times its own frame mean against the beam's 5.9, and by the swarm picture. Nothing downstream leans on the cell: the mixture's case rests on the ring's 545.7 and on the arc's tail, both far outside their noise.</p>
      <p>Strategy <b>30</b> puts all three on one measure — arclength along the camera ray — and applies the balance heuristic from the section above. Two properties fall out. With one shared kernel radius the <span class="mono">2r</span> cancels identically in the weights, so the shared-width instinct buys the densities for free. And the contribution goes as <span class="mono">1/sin ψ</span> while the weight goes as <span class="mono">sin ψ</span>, so the product is bounded and at most one member can be grazing-singular at a time. No member has to hand its tangent in: the three tangents are just the spherical frame rebuilt at the crossing.</p>

      <h3>Consistent, not unbiased — and the number that says how much</h3>
      <p class="note" style="border-color:var(--l680)"><b>The caveat travels with the estimator.</b> At a finite radius the three members deposit on manifolds a radius apart, so the balance weights do not sum to one pointwise. <b>Strategy 30 is CONSISTENT, NOT UNBIASED.</b> It converges as <span class="mono">r → 0</span> and nowhere else, and it must never be called unbiased. That is also why complement mode refuses it as a primary <em>by name</em>: comp mode's claim is that the two layers together are the whole transport, and a primary that owns a box and then mis-reports its total breaks that claim at every radius the mode is actually run at. Its two members, 28 and 29, are each a single blur-biased estimator of the same box exactly as the beam is, and are not refused.</p>
      <p class="note" style="border-color:var(--l680)"><b>Since 2026-09-09 the caveat has a mechanism.</b> The kernel half-width is one control for the whole biased family &mdash; the <b>Blur width</b> group, shown for every strategy whose class-table kernel bit is set (18, 19, 21, 28, 29, 30) and for the surface map, hidden for the kernel-free rest &mdash; and it carries an opt-in progressive shrink, <span class="mono">pp.blurShrink</span> = &beta;, under which pass <i>i</i> reads <span class="mono">pp.beamRadius</span>&middot;<i>i</i><sup>&minus;&beta;</sup>. Within a pass every member of 30 still shares one <i>r</i>, so the 2<i>r</i> cancellation above is untouched; the running mean over passes is then the progressive estimator, and &ldquo;consistent as <i>r</i> &rarr; 0&rdquo; is something the renderer does rather than something the spec says. Measured on this preset at <i>r</i><sub>0</sub> = 0.30, &beta; = 1/3: the trio&rsquo;s excess over the beam plateau falls from +0.0036 &plusmn; 0.0003 at a fixed radius to inside the protocol&rsquo;s bar by 6,400 passes, and the ring&rsquo;s from 0.34 to 0.0125 &plusmn; 0.0046, with the derivation&rsquo;s &beta;* = 1/(2<i>p</i>+<i>d</i>) = 1/3 for the tube consistent with the observed slopes. Default &beta; = 0: every existing film is byte-identical. The numbers and their producer are in <span class="mono">docs/ring-arc/SPEC.md</span> &sect;3.1 and <span class="mono">docs/tools/blur_shrink.py</span>.</p>

      <p><b>At r = 0.30, where the ring alone is 33 % high at eighteen sigma, the mixture is 0.33 % high — a hundredfold reduction, both terms measured, neither inferred.</b> And the 0.33 % is itself resolved at <b>7.5 sigma</b>, which is a change of verdict in the direction that costs us: with a per-rung bar the mixture <em>is</em> measurably biased at the widest kernel. That 7.5 replaces a shipped 5.1, and the correction runs the way that hurts. The 5.1 assumed the mixture and the beam were independent; batch <span class="mono">k</span> of every cell is the same block of pass indices, so they are not, and the measured batch correlation there is <span class="mono">ρ = +0.57</span> — the sign the construction predicts, since 30 <em>contains</em> the beam as an MIS member. A positive correlation means quadrature over-states the differential bar, so the shipped label was conservative and the honest one makes the bias more resolved, not less. That is the caveat above showing up as a number instead of a warning, and it is the right number to publish beside the payoff, because the payoff is unharmed. Below r = 0.15 the mixture is indistinguishable from the beam at every rung.</p>
      <p>The ring's excess, fitted rather than eyeballed over the four resolved rungs — 0.30, 0.15, 0.075, 0.0375, where the <em>whole-frame</em> excess clears twice its standard error — goes as <span class="mono">r<sup>1.067</sup></span> (+0.105 / −0.096, χ² 0.20 on 2 dof) — first order. <b>How far second order lies depends on the yardstick, and the yardstick is now named.</b> The headline is the profile-likelihood statistic — <span class="mono">√(30.67 − 0.20)</span> = <b>5.5 sigma</b>, delta-chi-squared — chosen because it evaluates the fit <em>at</em> p = 2 instead of extrapolating to it. The linear reading, (2 − 1.0670)/0.1055 = <b>8.8 sigma</b> — from the <em>unrounded</em> fit, which is the convention; dividing the rounded p and sigma gives 8.9, and that difference is arithmetic — is quoted for continuity and is no longer the one to cite: it runs a 68 % Monte-Carlo interval nine of its own widths into a tail it was never fitted on. Neither is a limit of the data — 0 of 20,000 refits reach p ≥ 2.</p>
      <p><b>Why first order rather than second is not settled, and the mechanism this page used to give for it has been withdrawn.</b> The obvious explanation is that a whole-frame average is dominated by the pixels where the ray is near coplanar with the ring's circle, where in general position the numpy model measures 2.4. That explanation needs the near-coplanar band to be the <em>low</em>-exponent region — low is what drags a frame average down to first order — and fitting the band and the rest of the frame separately puts the band's exponent <em>at or above</em> the rest's under both fitting conventions: 1.314 (+0.172 / −0.158) against 1.225 (+0.206 / −0.204) over the rungs resolved <em>within each subset</em> (three of them — 0.30, 0.15, 0.075 — the same rule as the whole-frame fit but on wider bars, so one rung fewer), and 1.571 (+0.121 / −0.105) against 1.322 (+0.155 / −0.144) forced over all five. The mechanism failed its own test, so it is withdrawn rather than softened. <b>What survives is the concentration, and it is measured</b>: the 5 % of pixels with the largest excess carry <b>64.5 / 63.5 / 60.8 / 58.2 %</b> of the frame's whole excess at the top four rungs, selected on four batches and scored on the other four so that the selection is not the noise being scored. The honest reason the split cannot decide it is that the mask is frozen at the <span class="mono">r</span> = 0.30 geometry while the real band narrows with <span class="mono">r</span>, so by 0.019 it holds 4.3 % of the excess instead of 60 %. A band that tracks <span class="mono">r</span> would be the experiment; it has not been run.</p>
      <p>Three labels moved when the bars were measured instead of carried, and all three are recorded: the &ldquo;unresolved&rdquo; mark on <span class="mono">r</span> = 0.0375 is <b>retracted</b> (1.5 sigma against a transplanted bar, 3.6 against its own); the arc's suspicious non-monotone rung turns out to be 0.20 sigma — it was the bar all along; and the ring's bottom rung, once &ldquo;exactly on the 2 sigma line&rdquo;, reads <b>1.7 sigma</b> once the batch correlation is measured rather than assumed away (<span class="mono">ρ</span> is <em>negative</em> for the ring, −0.44 at the top of the ladder, so quadrature was anti-conservative there). Unresolved rather than borderline. The paired ladder reads 17.8 / 6.9 / 4.5 / 3.5 / 1.7 for the ring and 13.8 / 3.7 / 1.6 / 1.4 / 0.1 for the arc, every other verdict unchanged, and <b>the exponent does not move at all</b> &mdash; <span class="mono">p</span> = 1.0670 on the independence-assuming bars and 1.0673 on the paired ones, 1.067 either way. (Until 2026-09-08 this sentence ended &ldquo;and the exponent moves by 0.001&rdquo;. That 0.001 was a grid artefact and not a measurement: <span class="mono">ra_fit.py</span> profiled <span class="mono">p</span> on a 0.0025-wide grid and reported its node 1.0675 as 1.068, while <span class="mono">ra_p2.py</span> used a grid ten times finer. Both scripts now share the fine grid, and <span class="mono">harness/ra_gate/p2.json</span> stores the whole derivation.) The caveat travels with it: eight batches give <span class="mono">ρ</span> a 68 % width of about ±0.45 and the paired standard error itself is good to about ±27 %, so neither 1.7 nor the 2.0 it replaces should be read to two digits — the point is that the rung is not resolved and that the shipped bar was the wrong estimator, not the wrong digit.</p>
      <p class="note">The whole column sits about 2 % above the oracle, and that offset is <em>shared</em>, not a property of the new members: the beam's row is flat in the radius at χ² 1.17 on 4 dof. It was tested on four fronts that could each have killed it. The sharp one is strategy 12, three-plane MIS, which has no kernel at all, finds its emitter by a different route, and renders byte-identically with the photon-lamp convention flag on and off — it cannot even read the flag the sweeps use, and it reads the same <b>1.0198 ± 0.0007</b>. Change the lamp and both move together, to 1.0336 and 1.0338 on preset 62. The statistic moves 1.4 percentage points across the light-size series, fourteen times its own bar, so it has discriminating power. The offset is a convention residue, it is preset-dependent, and it is not claimed to be zero.</p>

      <h3>It loses to the plain beam on easy transport, and per draw it only matches it</h3>
      <p>Equal time, same rig: <b>290 ± 14 for the mixture against 116 ± 7 for the beam alone</b> — a loss of <b>2.51 ± 0.19×</b>. The mixture beats the two sweeps it contains — <b>1.52 ± 0.10×</b> the ring, <b>3.87 ± 0.40×</b> the arc, and a factor of ten on the arc's p90 tail — and it loses to the member that was already there. All three orderings survive their bars, which is why they are quoted with them. That is stated in the ledger and not buried. It is consistent with the family's thesis — <b>these primitives are heavier than plain sampling, and they win on hard paths, not easy ones</b> — but consistent is not confirmed: this group's hard case, a sharp emitter or an occluded lamp, has not been run, and until it is, the 2.51× loss is the whole of the measured verdict.</p>
      <p>Single scatter in clear fog with nothing in the way is the easiest transport this renderer has. The beam is the one member of the group that is singular nowhere, it costs 1.073 ± 0.062 ms per pass against the sweeps' 3.5–7.4, and on a rig with no obstruction it is very hard to beat — the same way the camera walk's next-event estimate beats every swept member of the airlight family one section up. What the mixture buys is not speed on that rig. It is that <b>it never has a worst pixel</b>, which is the property that matters the moment the geometry stops being easy: the ring's worst pixel is 545× its own frame mean and the arc's is 42×, while the mixture's is 5.0 — under the beam's own 5.9. A pixel like the ring's is not fixed by more passes. The ring and the arc sit inside the camera walk's own cost class — the walk costs 3.410 ± 0.567 ms per pass here, and they are 1.22× and 1.04× that — while the mixture is 2.2× the walk; the beam is 3.3× cheaper than the cheapest sweep, and the mixture is the only one you can point anywhere. <b>How well is that column known? To about 10 % within a session and about 1.6× between them.</b> It was re-measured on 2026-09-08 as three interleaved ladders on one binary, and the bars above are the spread of those three. The other number that has been quoted for it — a slope over the same eight batches' stored wall clocks, 0.73 / 6.90 / 3.38 / 15.13 — is not a competing measurement and not merely “load-contaminated” in the abstract: three of its four fits want a <em>negative</em> process start (−4.5 s, −5.1 s) where a real one is +0.3 to +0.9 s, two of its ladders are not monotone in the pass count, and at a matched 3,200 passes its renders ran anywhere from 0.61× to 2.05× the speed of tonight's — the machine moved inside the single sequential run that produced it. It does <em>not</em> swap the ring and the arc: that claim was wrong and is withdrawn, and all three measurements order the four members identically. Every ladder point now lives in <span class="mono">ra_gate/cost.json</span>.</p>

      <h3>What it looks like</h3>
      <p>The <b>frozen batch</b> renders the whole one-dimensional group as geometry rather than as a film: twelve primitives per member, the sample pool frozen, 400 passes. Strategy 28 draws a swarm of <b>nested circles seen edge-on</b> — twelve rings coaxial about the lamp normal, stacking into horizontal arcs of increasing radius, brightest where the sweep is slowest. Strategy 29 draws twelve <b>open hooks</b>, half-circles about the vertex leaning at twelve different azimuths instead of stacking. Strategy 30 draws both of those <em>plus</em> straight streaks: rings, hooks and beams in one frame, and the trio is legible as a trio. No code was needed for any of it — the correlated-batch reconstruct path re-seeds after the camera prefix and calls the ordinary per-pixel sampler, which is strategy-agnostic by construction.</p>
      <p>The converged frames make the same argument a second and more useful way. At 3,200 passes the ring's film carries a <b>bright horizontal band of fireflies across the middle of the frame</b> — the locus where the camera ray is coplanar with its circle, exactly where the derivation says it must be. The arc's film carries a bright patch low and centre, on its own axis locus. <b>The mixture's film has neither.</b> The band and the patch are gone and what is left is the smooth fog gradient the beam and the oracle render. That is the disjoint-tangency argument as a photograph instead of a table.</p>

    <div class="narrow">
      <h3>What the gates checked, and what was deliberately left out</h3>
      <p>The derivation gate was <b>re-run rather than inherited</b>, at 800,000 samples per cell against a composite Gauss-Legendre reference the estimators share no code with, and then again against an adversarial independent estimator that shares none of the algebra either — no quartic, no root find, no Jacobian, no <span class="mono">sin ψ</span>, just a tube indicator integrated by deterministic quadrature. The two rows that gate quotes &mdash; <span class="mono">A_broadside</span> against the isotropic lobe and against the tilted one &mdash; land within 1.2 sigma of unity on all three members. <b>Storing the adversary's whole twelve-row table on 2026-09-08 showed what quoting two rows had hidden</b>: ten of the twelve sit within about 5 % of unity for all three members and <b>two do not</b>. <span class="mono">B_steep/lobeT</span> reads 11.65 / 9.99 / 10.22 and <span class="mono">C_neartang/lobeA</span> reads 1.58 / 1.49 / 1.72. The disagreement is <em>common to all three members</em> &mdash; they agree with each other to about 15 % &mdash; which is not the shape of a member's Jacobian being wrong, and it is the only thing this estimator was built to test; both cells are anomalous against the quadrature reference too, and <span class="mono">B_steep/lobeT</span>'s reference is 4.549e-07, three to four orders below every other cell in the table. That reading is <em>inferred</em>: the settling experiment &mdash; shrink the tube, refine the quadrature, watch the two sides converge &mdash; has not been run, so those two cells are reported unresolved and <b>the adversary's verdict stands on the other ten</b>, not on all twelve. The <b>21 pinned films are byte-identical</b> across the change, and the pre-change set was itself verified 21/21 against the last recorded run before a line was written, so the baseline is a measurement and not an assumption. The bias-ladder film and the eight-batch film are 20/20 byte-identical, so the error bars cost no centre.</p>
      <p>Two panel behaviours are load-bearing here and worth naming. Every intersection the group makes runs through the estimator's shared options, so a <b>direction-preserving surface</b> is transparent to all three members: a passthrough wall lets the camera stand outside the fog box without any of them noticing, and unlike the three photon walks the group has no walk to relaunch, because it is a single segment from the light. And the group's shadow ray is applied to all three members rather than to the one that happens to need it, which is load-bearing rather than tidy: the supports must agree or the balance weights would be weighting a path class that one member cannot reach.</p>
      <p>When a strategy's scene requirement is not met, <b>the refusal is printed</b> under the Strategy combo in orange — what this scene lacks and what would fix it — rather than left in the log, because the symptom of an unmet requirement is a black frame and nobody hovers a control that appears to be working. The one-dimensional group declares that it needs both scene lights and a medium. Complement mode's <b>live ownership read-out</b> sits beside it and prints the resolved partition from the same declaration the runtime uses; it is the read-out whose needs bitmask was corrected on the <b>volume-light rig</b>, where two strategies had declared scene lights while their bodies actually looked for an emissive volume, so preset 43 would have been refused for the wrong reason.</p>
      <p class="note"><b>Not in version one, and said so plainly.</b> Single scatter only — all three are the first segment out of the light, which is why the mixture's beam leg is written locally rather than calling the shipped multi-bounce walk. No surface bounce yet, though the evaluated payload above is exactly the property that would allow one. No flexible subset dial: 30 is the fixed trio, and a mask for it would be four lines nobody has needed. And <span class="mono">Tr(t)</span> assumes the medium is homogeneous between the vertex and the crossing, which is exact for every fog box these run on. The obvious optimisation — a closed-form quartic solve in place of fifty bisection iterations — was <em>not</em> attempted, because the bisection is the version that is provably right and the cost is not out of range. <b>Which members that covers is a correction.</b> On the re-measured column &mdash; three interleaved replicates, <span class="mono">harness/ra_gate/cost.json</span> &mdash; the ring and the arc are 1.22&times; and 1.04&times; the camera walk's own 3.410 &plusmn; 0.567 ms per pass, inside its class; the <em>mixture</em> is 2.2&times; it. Until 2026-09-08 this page put all three inside the walk's class, on a cost column that had no stored artifact behind it.</p>

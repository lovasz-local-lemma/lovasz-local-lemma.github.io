<section id="residue" style="--secc:var(--accent)" data-nav="The residue">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="narrow">
      <p class="sec-label">01 &mdash; What a curated site leaves out</p>
      <h2>Ten threads is a selection. This is the complement</h2>
      <p class="lead">Every other page here argues something. This one does not: it is an inventory, and its only claim is that the inventory is complete enough to be worth publishing. The ten threads were chosen because each had a mechanism or a number behind it. Choosing has a cost, and the cost is that a reader cannot tell what was passed over from what does not exist.</p>
      <p>So the rule this page applies to itself is stated up front, and it is a rule about <em>refusal</em> more than about coverage:</p>
      <p class="note"><b>A feature earns prose only where the site can say something about it that its own name does not already say</b> &mdash; a number, a mechanism, or a defect. Everything else earns a table row: name, one clause, grade. An absence earns a row and never a paragraph, unless the absence corrects something the site has already claimed. And a control that does nothing earns a line, because saying so is the editorial position rather than an omission.</p>
      <p>Applied honestly that rule mostly says no. The photon-primitive family, bidirectional transport and the camera all pass it, and all three already have pages; nothing here is going to out-argue them. What is left below is a projector mode with a real mechanism, a spectral pipeline with one specific hole in it, thirteen half-built modules, and several hundred rows that reduce to a clause each.</p>
    </div>

    {{SVG:f30-subsystem-map.svg}}
    <div class="narrow">
      <p class="note" style="margin-top:18px"><b>Reading the map.</b> One scene description feeds two render paths that share nothing else, and eight subsystems hang off them.       <p>The exhaustive version is not here and should not be. <span class="mono">docs/FEATURE-CATALOGUE.md</span> carries all 555 rows with a source anchor on each &mdash; 253 seen, 116 never viewed, 97 measured, 31 inert, 24 absent, 18 superseded, 16 scene-dependent. <section id="projector" style="--secc:var(--l400)" data-nav="The other projector">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="narrow">
      <p class="sec-label">03 &mdash; Six modes, four destinations</p>
      <h2>The projector that bakes, and the projector that lights</h2>
      <p class="lead">There are two projectors in this renderer and they are unrelated. The first is the structured-light lamp inside the megakernel &mdash; four delta geometries, seven patterns, a time-of-flight carrier &mdash; and thread 05 is about it. The second lives in the node graph and has six modes. Thread 05 names three of them in a paragraph and thread 07 has an unshot clip of the bake; what no page has said is <em>where each mode lands</em>, and that is the fact the mode combo hides.</p>
      <p>Wire a <b>Camera</b> node, which freezes the live view-projection at one button press, and a <b>Texture</b> node into a <b>DataMosh Projector</b>, and a photograph is cast into the scene from that frozen pose. The combo box beside it offers six modes, and they land in four different places &mdash; which is the whole reason the mode matters more than any slider under it.</p>
    </div>

    {{SVG:f31-projector-modes.svg}}

    <div class="narrow">
      <h3>The baking half</h3>
      <p>Mode 5 is the only one that leaves something behind. The rasteriser renders the projection <em>into texture space</em>: for every mesh with a layer in the tracer's baked-albedo array, it binds that layer as a framebuffer attachment, draws the mesh through a UV-space vertex shader at the chosen resolution, and writes the projected photograph into the texels the mesh covers. There is deliberately no clear &mdash; texels the projection misses keep the tracer's own material fill, so a bake paints onto a surface rather than replacing it.</p>
      <p>Three controls belong to that mode alone. <b>Bake res</b> runs 64 to 4096 and, because a high-resolution bake is slow, it does not re-bake while you drag: a separate <b>Re-bake</b> button commits it. <b>Bake side</b> is a three-way choice &mdash; the side facing the projector, the side facing away, or both &mdash; which is the question a decal system has to answer and usually answers silently. The result is not a decal or an overlay: those layers become entries in the array the path tracer samples for albedo, so the photograph is lit, shadowed and reflected like any other surface colour.</p>
      <p class="note"><b>Two limits worth stating in the same breath.</b> No CPU integrator sees any of the six modes &mdash; every projector uniform is written by the megakernel's uploader and the rasteriser, and by nothing else, so the entire projector surface is GPU-only in a renderer whose research half is on the CPU. And one function that looks like the CPU's way in, <span class="mono">sampleProjectorBake</span>, has zero callers anywhere, although the header comment above it describes the CPU material bakes calling it per texel.</p>
    </div>

<!-- ================= 04 · SPECTRUM ================= -->
<section id="spectrum" style="--secc:var(--l460)" data-nav="Spectrum">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="narrow">
      <p class="sec-label">04 &mdash; A curve for reflectance, none for emission</p>
      <h2>The spectral pipeline, and the one field it is missing</h2>
      <p class="lead">Spectral rendering here is whole, and all of it but one piece runs on the GPU. A hero wavelength is drawn per path, uniformly across the visible band, carried through the bidirectional connection and drawn again for the light-tracer path; conversion home is the CIE 1931 curves. Reflectance is up to eight Gaussian lobes per material, edited on a rainbow canvas that replaces the ordinary colour picker, packed into the material buffer and read back in the shading module. Spectral mode compiles its own kernel variant. Thread 04 covers that machinery, and it is the part of it that <em>works</em>.</p>
      <p>What has no spectrum is <strong>emission</strong>. The scene material carries a field named <span class="mono">spectralEmission</span> with a full spectral-curve type behind it, and that field has exactly one reference in the entire program: its own declaration. The consequence is visible rather than theoretical &mdash; in spectral mode an emitter falls back to its <em>albedo</em> curve, or to plain luminance when it has none. A spectral light source in this renderer borrows the spectrum it reflects with. A second field, <span class="mono">spectralSPP</span>, was declared for wavelength samples per pixel and has the same one reference.</p>
      <p>One piece of the spectral work is not GPU-only, and it is the piece the research half needed: dispersion. The CPU estimator evaluates the identical Cauchy expression per colour channel, anchored at the sodium D line, written deliberately verbatim from the shader so the two cannot drift &mdash; which is why the dispersive caustic on thread 01 can be compared against the megakernel at all.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

      <h3>Three strays that belong nowhere else</h3>
      <p>A <b>standalone crossing harness</b> is a single translation unit that links nothing from the renderer and re-implements the general refraction chain's per-pixel crossing exactly as its specification describes it, so the cost of a design can be measured before it is built. It was written because the specification had <em>withdrawn</em> an earlier cost estimate whose two factors were both assumed, and then forbade replacing it with another guess. Its verdict on the off-axis pair, at the same resolution the rest of the family is measured at, is that the general chain would cost 14 to 42 times the one-ball estimator per pass &mdash; so that tier is a no-go, and the harness is the reason it was never started.</p>
      <p>Two presets carry a correction in their own menu label rather than in a document. <em>Jellyfish Giant (FBM, not SmoothLife)</em> renames itself after the thing it actually builds. <em>Caustic Room (enclosed, roam inside)</em> says in its own entry that it must be viewed from within, which is the answer to the question its earlier name provoked every time somebody rendered it from outside and got a black frame.</p>
      <p>Two more of the same kind sit in the scene layer rather than the estimator: <span class="mono">vclGateGeom</span> and <span class="mono">vclGoboSlab</span>, the shared aperture vocabulary the gate presets are built from &mdash; four apertures across five scenes: a slot, an annulus, a grille and a dispersive slit, all derived from one light position, one ball and one gobo height rather than typed in as coordinates. That is why the annulus can be said to match the slot's aperture area to 1.4 %: the two were computed from the same geometry, not eyeballed into agreement.</p>
      <p class="note" style="border-color:var(--muted-2)"><b>What this page does not do.</b> It does not reprint the catalogue, and it does not re-grade anything: wherever <span class="mono">docs/FEATURE-CATALOGUE.md</span> carries a row, the grade shown here is that row's. Two entries have no catalogue row and are graded here for the first time &mdash; the second holography declaration, and the projector's mode-by-mode destinations. The <em>counts</em> are a different matter and were re-derived rather than copied, which is how the orphan-header figure moved from the catalogue's ten to fourteen. It does not claim the residue is complete: 551 is a floor, and the catalogue says so itself, because the 157 image operators are filed as 22 grouped rows and the 31 estimator slots as a far smaller number of grouped ones. And it does not photograph anything &mdash; the two schematics above were drawn, and the other five slots are four screenshot recipes and a clip that will stay recipes until somebody runs the program and follows them.</p>

<section id="coupling" style="--secc:var(--l400)" data-nav="The coupling">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="narrow">
      <p class="sec-label">01 &mdash; Where simulation meets transport</p>
      <h2>Three routes carry simulated state into the light transport; the best of them turns a fluid into participating media, and each has a limit worth naming</h2>
      <p class="lead">The test of a renderer with a physics engine inside it is not whether the physics runs. It is whether a photon can see the result. Here it can, by three genuinely different mechanisms &mdash; and the strongest of the three is not the one the UI advertises.</p>
      <p>The advertised route is the <b>Physics</b> density source: a button in the volume properties panel whose tooltip reads &ldquo;Euler/SPH/MPM fluid density from physics simulation.&rdquo; That tooltip is a mislabel and the catalogue records it as one. <span class="mono">uploadPhysDensityTexture</span> reads <span class="mono">m_physics.eulerDensity()</span> and nothing else, and <span class="mono">m_eulerDensity[&hellip;]</span> is written only inside the three Euler routines. Choose SPH, MPM, Simple Particles or Soft Body and the grid stays at the zeros <span class="mono">init()</span> filled it with, so the volume renders empty.</p>
      <p>The <em>unadvertised</em> route is the real coupling. When <b>Show Particles</b> is on and the solver is SPH or MPM, every particle is pushed into the tracer&rsquo;s primitive buffer as a real volume sphere &mdash; radius 0.03, &sigma;<sub>s</sub> 20, &sigma;<sub>a</sub> 2, isotropic phase, material 0. These are not an overlay. They are scene primitives that shadow rays intersect, that appear in mirrors, and that scatter along every bounce. It is the only place in the renderer where a particle simulation becomes participating media.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="narrow">
      <p class="note"><b>The coupling, and its edge in the same breath.</b> Three of the five solvers reach the traced image &mdash; two by particle injection, one by the density grid &mdash; and they reach it on the GPU megakernel, the renderer that runs in real time, not on any of the other eight integrators. The remaining two, Simple Particles and Soft Body, reach it by no route at all; their output is the GL point overlay and nothing else. So &ldquo;five physics solvers coupled to a path-traced medium,&rdquo; the phrase on the landing page, is exact for three of them on one renderer, and this is the page that names the other two.</p>
      <p>The injection route also costs more than it looks. <span class="mono">invalidateScene()</span> is called on the frame the particle list changes, which rebuilds and re-uploads the entire primitive SSBO; while the simulation is running that is every frame. And the GPU intersector is a linear scan, with a second full scan per bounce for the volume interaction, so the SPH <b>Dam Break</b> preset&rsquo;s 2,000 particles are 2,000 extra entries in a list that is walked twice on every bounce of every path. This is a physically correct visualisation mode rather than a production path &mdash; and physically correct is the part that matters in a renderer whose subject is where light goes.</p>
    </div>

<section id="accum" style="--secc:var(--l460)" data-nav="Accumulation">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="narrow">
      <p class="sec-label">02 &mdash; What motion costs a progressive renderer</p>
      <h2>The engine has two answers to &ldquo;the scene changed&rdquo;, and the good one is a per-pixel adaptive accumulator</h2>
      <p class="lead">A progressive path tracer accumulates. Anything that moves invalidates what has been accumulated. This renderer solves it twice, with two different mechanisms; one of them is genuinely good, and the subsystem that gets it is not the one that needs it most.</p>
      <p><b>Animated volume noise gets the good one.</b> The <span class="mono">Animate Volumes</span> toggle does not reset accumulation. It changes the accumulator&rsquo;s weight from the uniform running mean 1/(n+1) to a per-pixel floor derived from how much that pixel actually changed:</p>
    </div>

    <div class="formula">
      <div class="f">diff = |c &minus; prev| / max(|prev|, 0.01)<br>w = max<span class="hl">(</span> 1/(n+1), &nbsp;mix<span class="hl">(</span> 1/64, 1/4, clamp(2&middot;diff, 0, 1) <span class="hl">)</span> <span class="hl">)</span></div>
      <div class="n">A pixel whose radiance is not changing keeps converging at 1/(n+1). A pixel that is changing falls back to a four-frame exponential memory. Verified at <span class="mono">pathTrace.comp:3750-3754</span>.</div>
    </div>

    <div class="narrow">
      <p>That is a genuinely good design, and the code says so out loud &mdash; the reaction-diffusion stepper carries the comment &ldquo;Do NOT resetAccumulation here &mdash; the adaptive temporal EMA in the shader handles the time-varying RD texture, keeping static parts smooth.&rdquo; The walls of an RD room converge to a clean image while the volume in the middle churns.</p>
      <p><b>Physics gets the blunt one.</b> The physics step sets <span class="mono">m_ui.needsAccumReset = true</span> unconditionally at the end of every stepped frame, and the render loop turns that into a full <span class="mono">resetAccumulation()</span> &mdash; the accumulation image is cleared and the frame counter returns to zero. So while a simulation is playing, every displayed frame is a fresh one-sample-per-pixel path trace, everywhere in the image, including the parts of the room that are not moving at all. The adaptive EMA that would have fixed exactly this is sitting in the same shader.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="readout">
      <div class="row"><span class="lbl">Animated volumes &mdash; accumulation</span><span class="val">never reset; per-pixel adaptive EMA</span></div>
      <div class="row"><span class="lbl">Animated volumes &mdash; fastest weight</span><span class="val">1/4 (four-frame memory)</span></div>
      <div class="row"><span class="lbl">Animated volumes &mdash; slowest weight</span><span class="val">1/64, or 1/(n+1) if that is larger</span></div>
      <div class="row"><span class="lbl">Reaction-diffusion &mdash; accumulation</span><span class="val">never reset; relies on the same EMA</span></div>
      <div class="row hi"><span class="lbl">Physics running &mdash; accumulation</span><span class="val">cleared every frame; effective 1 spp</span></div>
      <div class="row"><span class="lbl">Where the volume clock comes from</span><span class="val"><span class="mono">uTime = ImGui::GetTime()</span> &mdash; wall clock, not frame count</span></div>
    </div>

    <div class="narrow">
      <p>The wall clock in that last row has a consequence worth stating. Because <span class="mono">scene.nodeTime</span> is assigned <span class="mono">ImGui::GetTime()</span> unconditionally every frame, and the uniform prefers it over the frame-counter fallback, a slow render <em>skips</em> animation rather than slowing it. A heterogeneous volume that runs at 8 fps advances the same distance per second as one running at 60, so the EMA is averaging over larger jumps and the fog reads blurrier. The frame-counter fallback at <span class="mono">ComputePathTracer.cpp:1803-1805</span> would have made animation deterministic per frame; it is unreachable, because <span class="mono">nodeTime</span> is positive from the first frame onward.</p>
      <p class="note"><b>A second self-adjusting knob, in the other direction.</b> The physics stepper measures its own wall time and edits the UI. SPH and MPM get a minimum substep count computed from a target sub-timestep &mdash; 0.004 s for SPH, 0.002 s for MPM, hard-capped at 16 &mdash; written back into <span class="mono">m_ui.physSubsteps</span> so the slider visibly jumps up when you select a solver. Then, if a step takes longer than 16 ms, the substep count is decremented and written back again. Under load the simulation silently trades accuracy for frame rate and moves the slider while you watch. Honest, undocumented, and worth one clip.</p>
    </div>

<section id="solvers" style="--secc:var(--l500)" data-nav="Five solvers">

    <div class="narrow">
      <p class="sec-label">03 &mdash; The physics bank</p>
      <h2>Five solvers, sixteen seeding presets, and a sixth solver that steps every frame over an empty list</h2>
      <p class="lead">All five live in <span class="mono">PhysicsWorld</span> behind one <span class="mono">simType</span> integer, stepped from <span class="mono">Engine::update</span> with a substep loop. Four of the five are guarded by an emptiness check, so selecting a solver does nothing until something seeds it; Euler is the exception and steps unconditionally, which is part of why it is the one that ends up with a grid worth uploading.</p>
      <p class="note"><b>Sixteen is what the buttons come to.</b> Exactly sixteen named preset buttons exist &mdash; SPH 4, MPM 6, Simple Particles 6, all built from one <span class="mono">presetBtn</span> lambda at <span class="mono">UIManagerRender.cpp:5009&ndash;5123</span>. The Seeding column below also mentions SPH's default uniform grid and Soft Body's two shapes, which are not buttons; an earlier heading rounded the whole thing up to twenty, and twenty is not derivable from anything.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="tw"><table>

    <div class="narrow">
      <p class="note"><b>Reachability, not call sites.</b> The rigid-body row is the one case here that survives the audit rule that caught the light tracer. The <em>function</em> has a caller &mdash; it runs on every substep of every solver &mdash; so a naive grep grades it live. But the <em>feature</em> has no route: nothing anywhere constructs a <span class="mono">RigidBody</span>, no preset, no panel, no config key. Q1 is no, so this is genuinely absent, and it is absent while still costing a loop iteration sixteen times a frame.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="narrow">
      <h3>The Euler grid is seeded, then erased, on the same frame</h3>
      <p>This is a source read, not a measurement, and it comes with the one capture that settles it, because it bears directly on the claim the landing page makes.</p>
      <p>One press of Play, or any change that sets <span class="mono">physNeedsInit</span>, runs a single block in <span class="mono">Engine::update</span> in this order:</p>
    </div>

    <div class="formula">
      <div class="f">m_physics.reset()<br>m_physics.setEulerRes(<span class="hl">ui.physEulerRes</span>)<br>&hellip; seed the chosen solver &mdash; for Euler, <span class="hl">initEulerFluidTopEmpty()</span> fills the lower half<br>m_physics.<span class="hl">init(cfg)</span></div>
      <div class="n"><span class="mono">Engine.cpp:576-1030</span>. The last line is the problem.</div>
    </div>

    <div class="narrow">
      <p><span class="mono">PhysicsWorld::init</span> is four statements long. It stores the config, sets <span class="mono">m_eulerRes = 64</span> unconditionally, and reassigns both the density and the velocity vector to 64&sup3; zeros. So it runs after the seeding and undoes it: the resolution the slider asked for is replaced by 64, and the &ldquo;bottom half dense&rdquo; field <span class="mono">initEulerFluidTopEmpty</span> just built is replaced by zeros. <span class="mono">uploadPhysDensityTexture()</span>, three lines later, uploads that.</p>
      <p>Nothing puts density back. <span class="mono">stepEulerFluid</span> has no source term &mdash; it advects and dissipates what is already there, at 0.995 per step &mdash; and the wind injects velocity, not density. The only two routines that write density are the two initialisers, one of which is erased and the other of which, <span class="mono">initEulerFluidBlob</span>, has no caller anywhere in <span class="mono">src/</span>.</p>
      <p class="note"><b>What this predicts, and how to falsify it in one screenshot.</b> If the reading is right, a VolumeBox on the Physics density source renders empty for <em>every</em> solver, Euler included, and the Resolution slider is inert while still visibly restarting the sim &mdash; the same &ldquo;fakes a response&rdquo; class as the three photon-map controls on the catalogue&rsquo;s inert list. The test is the shot specified at the top of &sect;01: preset 0, a VolumeBox with density source Physics, Sim Type Euler Fluid, press Play, wait five seconds. Smoke means the reading is wrong and this paragraph should be deleted. An empty box means the advertised coupling has been broken by a four-line function called one line too late. Either way it is one capture, and it is the highest-value capture on this page.</p>
      <p>Three more Euler parameters sit just behind that. <span class="mono">eulerBuoyancy</span>, <span class="mono">eulerVorticity</span> and <span class="mono">eulerDissipation</span> each occur exactly twice in the entire source tree &mdash; the struct default and the read inside the solver. There is no slider, no config key and no writer, so buoyancy is pinned at 1.0, vorticity confinement at 0.5 and dissipation at 0.995 forever. The solver implements them properly; nothing can reach them.</p>
    </div>

    <div class="narrow">
      <h3>Wind is camera-driven, and its shape control is a drawing</h3>
      <p>Pressing <b>R</b> in FPS capture toggles a wind force whose origin is the camera position and whose direction is the camera forward vector. You aim it by looking. On the CPU path it adds a quadratic-falloff velocity impulse to the Euler grid cells within the radius, and separately pushes SPH, MPM and Simple particles; on the GPU particle path the same parameters go to <span class="mono">particleSim.comp</span> as uniforms.</p>
      <p>The Wind Blower panel offers a <b>Shape</b> combo &mdash; Cone or Cylinder &mdash; and a <b>Width</b> slider that only appears for Cylinder. Neither reaches the solver. <span class="mono">windShape</span> occurs in five places in <span class="mono">EngineRenderViz.cpp</span>, three in the panel, one in the header; <span class="mono">windWidth</span> in two, one and one. <span class="mono">applyWindForce</span> takes origin, direction, strength and radius, and the field it applies is a sphere. So the shape control changes the overlay you are shown and not the force you are applying, which is the same defect class as the physics-density tooltip: a widget that draws its own promise.</p>
      <p>One more asymmetry: the wind pushes SPH, MPM and Simple particles, and does not touch soft-body nodes. A soft body is immune to the wind.</p>
    </div>

<!-- ================= 04 · REACTION-DIFFUSION ================= -->
<section id="rd" style="--secc:var(--l540)" data-nav="Reaction&ndash;diffusion">
  <div class="wrap">
    <div class="narrow">
      <p class="sec-label">04 &mdash; The simulation nobody has seen</p>
      <h2>A real Gray-Scott simulation runs 64&sup3; cells a frame, drives density <em>and</em> colour, and is pre-empted three lines before it is read</h2>
      <p class="lead">This is the sharpest example on the page of a system that evolves state, feeds the transport, and is invisible &mdash; and unlike most such stories the mechanism is short enough to state in full.</p>
      <p>Loading any of the eight RD scene presets does four things. It applies a Gray-Scott parameter pair to <span class="mono">ReactionDiffusion</span>, initialises a 64&sup3; <span class="mono">RGBA16F</span> ping-pong pair seeded with five overlapping blobs plus noise, runs <b>500</b> warm-up steps so the pattern is already formed when the first frame draws, and builds a scene whose volume spheres carry <span class="mono">volNoiseScale = 51.0f</span> with the source comment <span class="mono">// Style 51 = RD 3D texture</span>. From then on the sim steps <span class="mono">rdSubsteps</span> times per frame &mdash; a 1 to 32 slider &mdash; and the result is bound as <span class="mono">uRDTexture</span>.</p>
      <p>Two shader branches read that texture. One turns the V concentration into density through a <span class="mono">smoothstep(0.05, 0.35, v)</span> with a boundary fade. The other &mdash; the better one &mdash; turns it into <b>coloured emission</b>: a hue cycled by V concentration and world position, masked by <span class="mono">pow(smoothstep(0.25, 0.5, v), 2.0)</span> and multiplied by 8.0, so the activated regions of the reaction glow and light the room.</p>
      <p>Both branches sit inside <span class="mono">if (!typeHandled)</span>. And the same scene builders that set <span class="mono">volNoiseScale = 51</span> also set <span class="mono">volumeType = ReactionDiffusion</span>, which is <span class="mono">volType == 2</span>, which is dispatched about eighty lines earlier and sets <span class="mono">typeHandled = true</span>. What you see in presets 5&ndash;8 and 10&ndash;13 is therefore <span class="mono">reactionDiffusionPattern3DNode</span> &mdash; a hand-tuned procedural <em>emulation</em> built from Worley cells, a sine fold and a domain warp &mdash; while the actual simulation runs, steps, and is thrown away.</p>
    </div>

      <h3>The route back, and why it exists by accident</h3>
      <p>There is a way to see the real simulation, and it is one click.       <p class="note"><b>Correction to a page already published.</b> <span class="mono">ten-integrators-one-scene.html</span> &sect;06 grades this row &ldquo;branch runs, result discarded&rdquo; and describes the investigation honestly: the branch was forced to <span class="mono">density = 0.0</span> and the probe re-run byte-identical, centre pixel 0.04631 either way, concluding &ldquo;something downstream throws its result away.       <h3>The ambient slider cancels itself, by construction</h3>
      <p>The two time-of-flight benches each synthesise a four-shot continuous-wave measurement per pixel and recover the phase from it. Both use the same two lines:</p>
    </div>

    <div class="formula">
      <div class="f">I<sub>k</sub> = clamp<span class="hl">(</span> &frac12; + &frac12;&middot;cos(&phi; + k&pi;/2) &nbsp;+&nbsp; <span class="hl">ambient</span> &nbsp;+&nbsp; <span class="hl">noise</span>, &nbsp;0, &nbsp;1.5 <span class="hl">)</span><br>&phi;&#770; = atan2<span class="hl">(</span> I<sub>3</sub> &minus; I<sub>1</sub>, &nbsp;I<sub>0</sub> &minus; I<sub>2</sub> <span class="hl">)</span></div>
      <div class="n">The ambient term is added identically to all four shots, so it cancels exactly in <em>both</em> differences.</div>
    </div>

    <div class="narrow">
      <p>Its only remaining route to the recovered phase is the clamp, and over the sliders&rsquo; full range the upper bound is unreachable: the largest a shot can be is 1.0 + 0.3 + 0.1 = 1.4, against a ceiling of 1.5. The lower bound at zero <em>does</em> bite, and only near a trough, and only when the ambient offset is smaller than the noise amplitude. So the one effect the Ambient slider has on a recovered depth is to <b>reduce</b> the error, by lifting the shots off the zero clamp &mdash; the opposite of what a control labelled Ambient reads as promising.</p>
      <p>The noise term is <span class="mono">(hash(uv, k) &minus; 0.5) &middot; 2 &middot; uNoise</span>: uniform on a symmetric interval and independent of the signal. Shot noise in a real sensor is Poisson and scales with the square root of the collected charge; this does not, so the slider is a fixed-amplitude dither wearing the name of a physical process. And neither shader takes a time or frame uniform, so the pattern is identical every frame &mdash; it is fixed-pattern noise, pinned to sensor <span class="mono">uv</span>, and accumulating passes will never average it away. That is the property that decides whether the multi-frequency unwrapping cascade beside it is being tested or merely decorated, and it is the reason this row is worth a paragraph rather than a line.</p>

The panel&rsquo;s own note is the correct summary and this is a footnote to it, not a contradiction.</p>

    <div class="narrow">
      <p class="sec-label">07 &mdash; Which of these actually evolve</p>
      <h2>The useful test is whether the thing reads its own previous frame</h2>
      <p class="lead">The image-space bank contains twenty operators filed under &ldquo;field-based and animated&rdquo; plus three under &ldquo;statistical physics and stochastic,&rdquo; and their names &mdash; Navier-Stokes, Schr&ouml;dinger evolution, Ising model, percolation, morphogenesis &mdash; all suggest state evolving over time. Most of them have none. The distinction is mechanical and cheap to check: does the shader sample a previous-frame texture? Note <em>sample</em>, not <em>declare</em> &mdash; two shaders here declare one and never read it, and both say so in the declaration. <a href="one-hundred-and-fifty-seven-ways.html#clock" style="color:var(--accent);text-decoration:none;border-bottom:1px solid var(--line)">The image-operator page</a> applies the same test to the whole display chain, NPR combo and tail included, and finds seventeen; this table is the twenty-three field-based and statistical-physics operators only, which is why the two numbers differ.</p>
      <p>Five do &mdash; four from those twenty-three, plus SmoothLife, which is NPR mode 13 rather than a bank toggle and is here because it is the cleanest example of the mechanism. The rest are pure functions of the current frame, re-derived from scratch every time it is drawn, which is why they hold still when the camera does and why they change discontinuously when it moves.</p>

      <p>The timeline row is the one to sit with. Somebody designed an animation system carefully &mdash; the target-path scheme is the right one, the Bezier handles are stored normalised, groups carry mute and lock &mdash; and then stopped before the implementation file. Every simulation on this page is consequently a live performance: you can drive it with the wind key, watch it, and lose it. That is why this page is mostly video placeholders, and why every one of them is a screen recording of a session rather than a render.</p>
      <p>Two of the rows above &mdash; the Euler solver and the soft body, both of which print <span class="mono">[WIP]</span> on their own panel &mdash; are also entries in the program-wide register of thirteen partly-built modules on <a href="everything-else.html#wip" style="color:var(--accent);text-decoration:none;border-bottom:1px solid var(--line)">the page for everything that did not get one</a>. That register counts them; this page is the census behind them, and neither repeats the other.</p>
      <p class="note"><b>What would change the picture.</b> Four of these gaps are one route each rather than one subsystem. Injecting Simple Particles alongside SPH and MPM in the tracer-visualisation path would take the coupled-solver count from three to four. Clearing <span class="mono">volNoiseScale</span> in Remove Source and adding the missing dispatch case would make the live Gray-Scott sim the thing the RD presets show. And moving the physics stepper from <span class="mono">resetAccumulation()</span> onto the adaptive EMA that reaction-diffusion already uses would let a simulated fluid converge everywhere it is not moving. None of the four is a research problem; each is a short edit to a route that already exists, and they are listed here so the next reader knows exactly where the coupling widens.</p>

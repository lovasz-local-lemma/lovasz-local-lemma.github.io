Visibility / The cost after the connection

Remember the openings.

Solving the connection does not tell us whether light can get through. A primitive can generate many candidate paths, only for walls to reject them. The expensive case is not simply “more bounces”: it is a family that moves several segments and must check them again.

Local families

A terminal beam, cone or sheet can keep most of a traced prefix. Validate the moved segment and its new connection; do not retrace an unchanged, already-valid prefix.

Separated coordinates

Generalized plane and rotational families can move intermediate vertices. Crossed sheets and independent medium bridges add their own joins. Count the affected segments, not just the number of bounces.

Structured media

What changes?

The path stays.
The solved coordinates move.

Two flight lengths and two azimuths describe the same short light path. Each method samples two coordinates and solves the other two together with the camera distance. The colored sheets are those solved families. The plane releases both distances. Try Shared blind direction, then switch the plane off: three decorative surfaces alone do not guarantee a well-conditioned estimator.

The cone uses the final azimuth and final distance. The cylinder moves the preceding distance. The hyperboloid moves the preceding azimuth. They use one consistent rotating frame.

The determinant J = |(Xᵤ × Xᵥ) · d| measures how transversely a sheet crosses the ray. Near tangency, J approaches zero.

For these bounded uniform proposals, balance weights are proportional to the residual sampling density times J. All valid isolated roots contribute.

A more intricate surface is not automatically a better estimator. The useful question is whether another chart covers its expensive or unstable connections.

Predict cheaply. Check selectively.

A small cache stores visibility on the primitive’s own coordinates. Cache-only rendering changes the answer. A randomized exact residual can restore its expectation, at the cost of additional variance—including negative individual samples.

Scope, assumptions and the published foundation

The coordinate atlas uses Canvas 2D projection of exact parameterized surfaces. Decorative glow is a display effect. The probe uses analytical quadric intersections; browser geometry is independently checked against derivatives and known paths. The recorded numerical experiments are separate Python implementations in RadianceLab’s research directory. Each of the six browser studies states its own computed quantities and remaining limits.

The live coordinate diagram and older conditional benchmark hold the two polar angles fixed and restrict both flight lengths to a finite interval. That benchmark checks a conditional integral without specular interfaces, occluders or a full heterogeneous transport ledger. The physical two-scatter films above come from a separate experiment with their own stated transport factors and limits. A general light-transport estimator still needs complete sampling densities, scattering and camera factors, transmittance, visibility, and path-class coverage.

The cone/cylinder/hyperboloid family follows the 2019 Photon Surfaces framework. The new experiment studies this small combination and its conditioning in the current project. No claim of a newly invented primitive or general rendering speed-up is made.

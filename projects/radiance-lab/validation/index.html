A preview is an approximation.

Finite angular resolution, screen filtering and a grazing floor introduce bias. The preview uses a pinhole camera and omits fog-sheet contributions seen through camera-side glass or mirrors. More passes reduce sampling noise; they cannot remove these differences. Images establish visible implementation output, not unbiasedness or universal speed.

01 / Preview

The cost of a faster view

Reference gathering and a biased rasterized preview share the same scene, light convention and angular geometry in each pair. The preview reduces camera work, interpolates shading and filters narrow folds. A ratio above 1 means the preview costs less per measured pass.

The preview film contains a high-intensity outlier and its mean differs substantially. With stage surfaces enabled, rare bright camera-side lamp paths in the stochastic surface companion can cause this pattern; the source is not causally isolated by these records. The native image and values are retained without clipping. This pair is not an accuracy certificate.

03 / Reproducibility

Every image has a record

The compact report contains original PNGs plus settings, timing and native-result JSON. Larger float films and native logs remain at the source paths recorded in the summary. PNGs are copied byte for byte; no local image adjustment is applied here.

Capture summary

Charts show CPU render-pass wall time. First-pass charts include geometry construction; steady charts exclude the recorded warmup. Paired runs share one executable and settings, while different studies may come from different binaries or capture sizes. The native timing record can name the OpenGL display device; that does not make the CPU preview GPU accelerated.

The ideaA renderer predicts the image a camera would see by calculating how light travels through a scene. This laboratory lets you change that calculation, the scene and the camera while inspecting the resulting image.

What to watchFollow how a change to a light, material or transport method changes the picture and the paths behind it.

The idea behind it

I wanted a renderer that felt intuitive to work in: walk into the scene, select what you see and edit it directly. You change the rendering technique freely and always see the live render preview; then you find a good spot, adjust your camera and take a photo, which gives you a high-quality render.

Bringing those pieces together took considerable work, but the result is now the environment I use for my rendering experiments.

I also wanted to see the work behind the image: auxiliary structures, intermediate results and sampled paths. Being able to inspect those details helps me test assumptions and trace a surprising result back to its cause.

This project allowed me to run rendering experiments much more efficiently and to get some really tricky techniques, including my own unpublished research, to work well. It has become both a personal graphics playground and a tool for my research.

01 · Transport methods · image formation · interactive tools

A rendering laboratory built for exploration

Description · at a glance

I wanted a renderer where I could explore a broad range of light-transport methods, build rich optical scenes and inspect the computation while using it. RadianceLab brings those three goals into one environment, from composing a finished image to examining how its light was estimated.

Ten selectable modes share the studio. The GPU tracer contains the path tracer, light tracer and bidirectional path tracer, while the photon-primitive mode contains a separate roster of 34 strategy slots. Rasterization, GPU compute, CPU-built/GPU-gathered photon maps and progressive CPU films provide different routes into the scene; the table identifies what each route currently implements, and the chapters that follow take the methods cluster by cluster.

ExplanationComprehensiveness means more than an integrator countMethods, image formation and day-to-day scene work are three complementary design goals.＋

The breadth pays off in the connections between capabilities: a difficult caustic can be composed visually, investigated through several sample constructions, and rendered with the same scene and camera context.

Ten is the number of top-level selectable modes, not ten independent, complete reproductions of established papers. MLT and PSSMLT currently both use primary-space mutation; UPBP (Unifying Points, Beams, and Paths) and manifold exploration have narrower implementations, described in their chapters. The source therefore supports a broad academic-methods laboratory with explicit experimental branches.

Real-time describes the intended interaction loop. The GPU material preview, a progressive CPU estimator and a converged high-resolution photograph have different costs. Work and resolution controls let those routes remain usable; no universal frame-rate or instantaneous-convergence claim is implied.

Photon primitives are listed as part of the transport dimension but get their own row, because they are a large system in themselves. The photon-primitive chapter (Mode 10) explains how a hand-picked set of swept dimensions becomes a technique, how techniques combine under MIS, and how stacked techniques cover every transport exactly once.

ExplanationA shared scene does not imply an identical estimatorThe rendering modes share an interface but retain their own material support, sampling rules and film outputs.＋

The difficult integration problem is not placing ten names in a selector. Each backend must agree with the application about scene edits, camera state, film ownership and when accumulated samples become invalid. A camera move must not blend old and new viewpoints. A material change must reach the actual transport implementation, rather than changing only the raster preview. A switch from GPU compute to a progressive CPU film also changes the producer of the displayed image.

RadianceLab makes these producers explicit. The GPU tracer contains the path tracer, light tracer and bidirectional path tracer in one compute shader. Conventional photon mapping builds photon data on the CPU and gathers it on the GPU. The other progressive modes maintain their own CPU films and diagnostic state. Consequently, equal pass counts do not establish equal work: a pass may launch camera paths, build a photon population, run a mutation batch or evaluate many swept primitives.

This separation is useful experimentally. A comparison can first match a supported diffuse or homogeneous-medium scene, then vary the estimator. More elaborate GPU materials and heterogeneous media remain valuable demonstrations, but they cannot serve as a reference for a CPU lane that does not implement the same model. The correct comparison is between matching image-formation contracts, not merely matching scene-file names.

ExplanationFeature specialization is part of the engineering resultPath construction, material support and shader size constrain which combinations can run together.＋

The GPU tracer specializes around transport features such as bidirectionality, spectral behavior, participating media and explicit path-density MIS. A single maximal shader is not automatically the best implementation: its instruction footprint can exceed the practical limits of the target OpenGL driver. The application therefore starts with a smaller configuration and selects specialized variants as features change.

One consequential boundary is instruction size. If a requested feature mask exceeds the driver budget, the explicit BDPT MIS variant is dropped and the failure is logged, rather than silently rendering a different estimator. Since the BDPT audit, a compact BDPT kernel keeps explicit MIS with one homogeneous medium; other media use a labeled volumetric path-tracing fallback, and finite-aperture camera BDPT still uses flat weights. These are visible feature boundaries, not evidence that surface MIS and medium transport separately fail.

The studio’s system contribution is the inspectable path from control to implementation: which backend runs, which variant was compiled, which film is accumulating, and which diagnostic buffers belong to it. Keeping those distinctions explicit prevents a visually plausible image from silently becoming evidence for a feature that was never enabled.

02 · Photon hourglass · analytical sphere chains

Volumetric caustics through glass, sharp within a second

Description · at a glance

Start with a cone of light. Continue every ray through a glass sphere, following entry and exit refraction, and the outgoing family becomes the hourglass. Its useful trick is to preserve an entire family of paths: the camera intersects a sheet instead of waiting for a nearby photon. The next question is natural: what if the incoming family is a plane?

Light focused by a glass ball and scattered once by fog toward the camera is one of the hardest paths to render: a shadow ray cannot bend through the glass, so a camera path finds this light only by chance, and never under a point light, while a photon beam or point kernel blurs it. The photon hourglass, strategy 20 of the CPU photon-primitive mode, follows both refractions through the sphere exactly and sweeps the exit ray around the axis from the light through the ball’s centre, producing a whole refracted sheet that narrows to a waist and fans out again. A camera ray crosses that sheet by solving a quadratic and collects its contribution at an exact weight, with no kernel radius. Here, photon hourglass means the analytical cone-through-glass construction on spheres and compatible chains. The polygon extension is called mesh sheets; it is kernel-free but uses a numerical surface representation.

It is fast. On the single-ball scene one pass costs 1.2 to 1.5 ms on a 217×140 film on laptop CPU cores, with no GPU compute in the loop. Measured at equal time against photon beams on the same caustic, its variance is 35 to 48 times lower on a two-ball chain and 17 to 21 times lower on a mirror relay. In the recordings the sheet under The Gate’s ball and under a collinear pair is sharp within a second of the camera stopping, at about 150 passes a second on a 524×386 film; restarted in place on the same caustic, photon beams are still grainy after three seconds and photon points barely outline it.

Pairing it with another estimator makes the point sharper. The hourglass owns the caustic, and something else draws the rest: mostly light that reaches the fog directly and scatters once, a far easier path. Yet in the recordings the easy part converges last. After the switch to the hourglass MIS pairing, the lamp’s direct glow is still grainy six seconds later, while the hourglass alone had smoothed the caustic within a second; on The Gate rendered with mesh photon sheets in the next chapter, the directly lit shaft needs two to three seconds after the caustic under the ball is already smooth.

Theory / mathematicsA cone through glass. What about a plane?The central plane can survive intact. Its cheap crossing is only part of the estimator.＋

For the spherical hourglass, fix the polar angle and sweep azimuth plus outgoing distance. Rotational symmetry turns the refracted family into a surface of revolution with a compact crossing solve. The complementary choice fixes azimuth and sweeps incidence angle plus outgoing distance: a meridian sheet through the light and sphere center. Every ray and surface normal stays in that plane, so both refractions preserve its planarity.

That makes the meridian an interesting candidate, not merely a less elegant hourglass. A camera-plane crossing is cheap, but the crossing point must still belong to the transmitted family. Recovering its incidence angle may require finding several branches near a caustic fold; the sampling density and Jacobian must count all valid paths. A nearly parallel camera ray also makes the plane poorly conditioned. This could complement the hourglass rather than replace it.

A parallel light sheet moved away from the sphere center tells a different story. Its varying normals bend it into a ruled, potentially folded sheet. A flat parallel-sided slab provides a useful control: a parallel ray sheet remains planar after its two refractions. The browser study computes these rays directly and draws their outgoing support and receiver footprint; its luminous shading is a display reconstruction, not a new unbiased transport estimator.

Performance depends on more than how easy the sheet looks to intersect. The existing spherical hourglass can rotate one solved meridian path; a general displaced sheet needs many distinct refraction evaluations and a finite support representation. Conversely, a central meridian offers a very cheap plane cull. Build cost, all-root inversion, visibility and error at equal time determine whether that trade pays. This browser study does not measure an equal-error speedup for the proposed volumetric meridian member.

There is already a related native implementation: surface receiver curves can use cone, meridian plane or a shared-budget mixture of both. That forward surface construction is distinct from the inverse camera-to-volume meridian estimator explored here. The new exhibit makes its geometry visible; it does not substitute for a transport benchmark.

A photon beam contributes only to camera rays that pass within its kernel radius, and a photon point only to rays that pass near the point. A refracted sheet is a surface in the fog, so a camera ray collects a contribution wherever it crosses it. In the numerical variance study, 74% of hourglass samples contribute to a fixed camera ray at the waist, against 11% for a beam of radius 0.01. At the waist that thin beam is as good per sample (relative variance 9.3 against 11.3), but below the waist, where the light bundle is wide, its relative variance grows to 92 against 14.5 for the hourglass. Fog density does not change those hit rates.

The equal-time figures come from the linear film, not the displayed image. On the collinear two-ball chain (preset 68), relative variance per pass times milliseconds per pass is 6.8 for the hourglass chain against 241 and 329 for beams of radius 0.12 and 0.06, and the total energy on the caustic agrees with the beam within about 0.6%. On the unfolded mirror leg (preset 71) the equal-time gain is 17.4 and 20.6 times.

Speed has a limit. Two four-ball chains under two lights (preset 77) carry a relative variance of 34 per pass on the caustic: 29% relative error at 400 passes, 15% at 1,600 and 7% at 6,400, which at 50 passes a second is about half a minute and two minutes of holding still. The recording of that scene, in the chains chapter, is still grainy after a few hundred passes, as measured.

Theory / mathematicsAnalytical hourglass and kernel-free mesh sheetsAn exact construction on spheres, a numerical extension to polygons, and a separate fast preview.＋

The analytical hourglass has an unbiased formulation for the path class it represents: exact refraction, all admissible crossings, the correct Jacobian and sampling density, and no discarded contributions. It estimates that class of transport; another estimator must supply paths outside its support. Mesh sheets extend the construction to polygonal geometry with traced rulings and numerical reconstruction. They avoid a photon-gathering kernel, but kernel-free does not by itself mean unbiased.

The recorded implementation has additional choices. A grazing guard rejects very small Jacobians, off-axis followers search a finite set of rulings, and strategy 21 introduces the finite-radius beam/point kernels of its MIS partners. These choices can introduce bias. The optional screen-raster viewfinder is a further, deliberately biased approximation. These are implementation distinctions, not different names for the same estimator.

03 · Eight ideas under exploration · selected successes and revealing failures

Rendering frontier: make the difficult connection easy

Description · at a glance

The hourglass suggests a broader question: what happens when the coordinates themselves follow a difficult connection? A rotational atlas combines cylinders, cones and hyperboloids, with a plane partner for a direction the three can share as a blind spot. A shadow atlas remembers visibility where neighboring primitive queries ask almost the same question.

The two-scatter suite carries a real point-source, phase-function, scattering, attenuation and visibility ledger. The shadow study evaluates an actual occluded sheet field. Alongside them, six smaller browser studies explore medium relays, arrival-time shells, tilted focus, nonlinear reflection constraints, plane–plane intersections and distant path coordinates. These are interesting ideas under exploration: an attractive working case and an instructive failure are enough to reveal their potential.

04 · Photon hourglass · the biased group

Beyond spheres: mesh photon sheets through triangles

The analytic hourglass needs a glass sphere. Mesh photon sheets (strategy 32) drop that requirement by tracing the sheet instead of deriving it. Suzanne, the Utah teapot, a torus with a hole and chains of different glass bodies all send their own waists and fans into the fog.

05 · Central research example

The hourglass: follow the refracted sheet

Description · at a glance

A source and a spherical dielectric define a rotational Snell construction. Sweeping the entry, internal chord and exit together produces a refracted sheet whose camera crossings recover the supported volumetric caustic.

The waist connects geometric focusing to estimator density. The core path has two specular transmissions followed by one medium-scattering event, with an index-one limit that supplies an independent check.

Theory / mathematicsThe hourglass sweeps an entire Snell chain by rotational symmetryIts analytic power comes from a precise geometric restriction.＋

Choose a source point outside a sphere and the axis joining it to the sphere center. A sampled polar direction determines one entry point, one internal chord and one exit direction through two Snell refractions. Rotating that entire path around the source–center axis preserves the sphere and both interfaces. The construction therefore sweeps a family of physically related refracted paths, rather than refracting independent rays and fitting a surface afterward.

After the second interface, sweeping azimuth and outgoing distance forms a ruled sheet. Its narrow waist is a geometric caustic: nearby members of the family approach one another. The camera query solves crossings of this sheet and evaluates the transmission, phase, attenuation and visibility belonging to the crossing’s actual azimuth.

The rotational restriction is essential. A general object or an off-axis follower does not preserve the same family under rotation. Even for a sphere, the implicit quadric contains algebraic branches that are not the forward transmitted ray. The valid nappe, outgoing distance and visibility checks define the estimator’s physical support. Setting the refractive index to one provides a sharp control: the construction should reduce to the corresponding cone family over the same sampled cap.

The hourglass paths are light → transmission at entry → transmission at exit → one volume-scattering event → eye, crossing two interfaces before a single medium event. The hourglass owns these paths and is stacked with other techniques into one estimator for complete transport (see Stacking techniques in the photon-primitive chapter).

Theory / mathematicsA cancelled MIS numerator is not the deposited sampling densityDimensional analysis catches an attractive but incorrect shortcut.＋

To compare the hourglass with a beam or point estimator, it is necessary to ask how the sampled polar coordinate deposits density along the queried camera ray. Sweeping two variables removes their random sampling, but the remaining map from polar coordinate to arclength still contributes a determinant. At a multi-branch crossing, the density sums all compatible branches.

The hourglass sheet Jacobian alone does not supply that arclength PDF. Multiplying it by the polar PDF produces a quantity with the wrong units. That product can appear after a shared three-dimensional determinant cancels between MIS alternatives, but the cancellation is local to a compatible path event and its support. Reusing the simplified numerator as an independent density outside that calculation is invalid.

This distinction also explains why a rough-interface NDF is not a substitute for the smooth hourglass construction. Exact Snell transmission constrains the half-vector at the interface; changing a roughness parameter around that constrained direction does not reproduce the geometric deposited density of the smooth refracted family. The older rough-proxy exploration remains a separate study, not the shipped smooth estimator’s derivation.

06 · From derivation to executable geometry

The useful constraint is rotational symmetry

Description · at a glance

Rotational symmetry reduces a camera crossing to a quadratic. The physical branch still needs validation, and near a fold the roots and their Jacobian must remain numerically consistent.

The revised solver shares stable algebra between root classification and weighting. Identical-draw replays, finite differences and limiting tests distinguish floating-point errors from genuine high-variance geometry.

A closed-form sheet is still a Monte Carlo estimator

The camera–sheet crossing can be solved analytically while source position, polar angle and other unswept variables remain sampled. A recognizable early frame is not a converged frame; no neural approximation or temporal denoiser is needed to explain the construction.

Theory / mathematicsThe solver and the estimator must agree near a double rootThe revised crossing routine stabilizes both the quadratic and the Jacobian derived from it.＋

Near a caustic fold, two ray–sheet crossings coalesce. The quadratic discriminant becomes small, so subtracting large nearly equal terms can invent a crossing or erase a real one. A separately evaluated Jacobian can suffer a second cancellation and assign an inconsistent weight to the root that survived. A firefly in this region can therefore come from genuine estimator geometry or from an avoidable floating-point inconsistency.

The September solver revision uses a common crossing routine with local double-precision arithmetic, while keeping the surrounding inputs and outputs in the renderer’s usual precision. It normalizes the axis, retains the actual ray-direction norm in the coefficients, shifts the ray parameter near the perpendicular foot relative to the apex, and evaluates a stable paired-root form. The Jacobian is derived from the same factored quantities as the discriminant.

These changes reduce cancellation; they do not introduce exact arithmetic or prove robustness for every possible scene scale. The documented comparison replayed identical draws against the prior solver and recorded root classification and energy changes. Keeping the draws fixed makes that test substantially more informative than comparing two unrelated noisy images.

The chain study consequently records both successful configurations and refusal conditions. Reflection-cone, exit-cone and glossy studies also vary the seed and path class. A glossy vertex adds real directional freedom, so treating it as an ideal mirror changes the problem. ExplanationMoving one follower changes the solver, not just the pictureA rigid rotational chain gives a quadratic. A general ruled chain requires root isolation on the actual refracted map.＋

On a common point-source axis, tracing one meridian gives the complete azimuth family by rigid rotation. A source displaced relative to one sphere alone does not destroy this symmetry: its source-to-center line simply becomes the axis.

A follower away from that axis is different. Its entry point, chord, exit point and outgoing direction vary nontrivially with azimuth; some azimuths miss it. The native off-axis solver samples rulings, brackets sign changes of the camera-crossing residual, refines on the true Snell chain, and evaluates a changing Jacobian with finite differences. Grazing root pairs can need an adaptive rescan. Repeated chain evaluation per queried camera ray accounts for much of the extra work.

The native off-axis implementation currently supports one displaced follower, with an uncorrelated query path; it does not have the same reusable correlated/raster bound as the axial chain. 08 · The tridirectional hourglass · for completeness

Change the seed, change which paths are easy

Description · at a glance

The tridirectional hourglass seeds the path on the glass itself: a point on the sphere, then two halves built independently, a light connection traced back to the lamp and a swept exit sheet that the camera meets. Light subpath, sensor subpath and the two-segment chain through the glass are joined at two constraints, which is what the name means; the project uses it for the whole hourglass family, and these are the members that make it literal. The hourglass began in this glass-seeded form, and the light-seeded strategy 20 replaced it: once the camera crossing fixes the azimuth, the light side has no freedom left and must reach the lamp by tracing, which almost never happens with a small lamp and never with a point light, and that first form carried only one of the ball’s two refractions. The vertex members bring the glass seed back with strategy 20’s machinery. Strategy 22 reuses 20’s Snell chain, camera solve and Jacobian with the rotation axis moved to the surface normal through the ball’s centre; the exit cone (23) and the reflection cone (24) are true cones with their apex on the glass, met with the cone solve of strategy 9.

They are less impressive, and the recordings show why. On the same ball and small lamp, strategy 20 draws a solid caustic horn by pass 60 while 22 and 23 still show sparse dots at pass 76. In the rendered lamp-size study, 20 beat 22 at every size tested, by 236 times at a 2.3° lamp down to 1.9 times at a 77° lamp, where the light trace succeeds almost every time and the fold of the sheet sets the remaining gap; the earlier numerical prediction that 22 would overtake 20 on large lamps did not hold in the renderer. The reflection cone (24) is the positive result: to my knowledge, the first analytic sphere-reflection member within the photon-primitive framework, matching the beam’s reflection branch at 0.998 and filling 1.5 times as many pixels, for lamps with an edge of about 1 or more.

The light-size study measures successful closure separately from nonzero weights. It shows why seed choice and light-side importance sampling matter more than adding every variant to one mixture.

A surface receiver needs another degree of freedom

The volumetric construction does not transfer to a wall by merely intersecting the photon sheet with it. That intersection is a curve on the receiver, while a fixed camera hit generally lands elsewhere. The proposed symmetric construction also sweeps a camera sheet, so two footprints can meet on the receiver with a co-area factor involving both sheet normals and the surface normal.

The implemented surface contribution is a simpler reference denominator: a Lambertian next-event connection at the camera’s opaque surface hit, without a continuation bounce in that mode. The two-sheet footprint is a mathematical extension described by the source, not an estimator claimed to be finished.

Theory / mathematicsChanging the seed moves the rare event to a different end of the pathA well-conditioned geometric weight can still lose to a low probability of reaching the emitter.＋

A light-seeded construction begins with a source sample and asks which refracted family reaches the camera ray. 09 · Mode 10 · the research family

Photon primitives: sweep, combine and cover the paths

Description · at a glance

Mode 10 contains the project’s original research: a multithreaded CPU progressive estimator with an optional full-resolution film. A photon primitive samples some coordinates of a light path and sweeps the rest into geometry, such as a beam, ring, sphere, cone, plane or refracted sheet, which the camera ray then queries.

The roster has 34 strategy slots, numbered 0–33. The table shows every way to sweep the direction angles and flight distance of one photon segment, and the emitter-coordinate sheets; further slots add refracted hourglass constructions and their chains, vertex-seeded and glossy variants, MIS mixtures, a rasterized gather, a user-picked pair or trio (31), mesh photon sheets (32) and surface curves (33).

Two ideas turn the roster into a system. Hand-pick the swept dimensions and the framework turns that choice into a technique, weighted under MIS against the techniques that share its measure. Stacking combines them without counting a transport contribution twice: MIS combines compatible overlapping strategies into one estimate, while a hard partition assigns the remaining path classes to a different estimator. The hourglass and a camera walk can then contribute to the same image without both counting the hourglass paths. RadianceLab is the most complete implementation of this photon-primitive framework I am aware of. Earlier reference implementations cover subsets; later work also extends particular parts. The claim concerns the range of primitive constructions brought together here, not universal support for every scene or light path. The relation to the later research

Photon Primitives changes the represented set from a stored point and its reconstruction kernel to beams, sheets or other analytically handled families. It is a separate top-level mode, not simply a display option for the conventional photon map.

ExplanationFrom hand-picked swept dimensions to a technique with MISHand-pick the swept dimensions: the framework classifies the primitive, evaluates it analytically and weights it under MIS, and stacking gives every transport exactly one owner.＋

Paths sampled from the light and from the camera have to meet, and a single meeting point has zero measure. Connecting the two endpoints fails at specular vertices; blurring them together adds bias. Photon primitives take a third route: sweep, that is integrate analytically, some of the random choices made while building a path. The endpoint then becomes a curve, sheet or volume that the other subpath crosses with nonzero probability. Any choice made during path construction can be swept: the direction angles θ and φ and the flight distance t at any vertex, the emitter’s surface coordinates, or the aperture sample on the camera side.

Once the swept dimensions are picked, the framework builds the technique. A primitive is a list of swept terms, each naming a dimension, the vertex it belongs to and whether it is integrated analytically or sampled. A classifier names the shape: θ and φ at one vertex give a sphere, φ and t a cone, θ and t a disk, all three a ball, two distances a plane, φ at one vertex with t at the next a cylinder, and three distances a parallelepiped. The matching intersector and Jacobian then evaluate it, always through one template: swept dimensions, manifold, crossing roots, Jacobian, contribution. Three free coordinates in total, split between the two subpaths in any way, give an unbiased connection; with fewer, a blur kernel is needed, which is where photon points and beams sit.

Established techniques belong to the same family. The UI names each technique by what it sweeps: L[θφT]=C, with all three coordinates swept on the light side, is NEE (equivalent to the photon ball), and naive path tracing sweeps the light’s own surface and meets the camera ray there. The Photon Primitive Editor draws the idea: toggle θ, φ and distance at any vertex of a camera or light subpath, and it names the shape, shades its Jacobian-weighted density and shows two chosen sweeps combined with the balance heuristic.

Every primitive is a sampling strategy, and techniques combine under MIS by re-evaluation: a primitive pretends to be another one, asks how that technique would have generated the same contributing path, and the balance heuristic weights the densities on a shared measure. Primitives of the same type combine too, such as three planes that sweep different pairs of distances, and so does a continuum of them: arbitrarily oriented planes are MIS-weighted analytically. The best partners behave most differently as the view changes; a photon sphere always has a bright rim, while a plane’s brightness depends on how it faces the camera ray. In the renderer the shared measures group emitter planes on the light-area measure; sphere, disk and cone conditional on the light point; beam, point and hourglass along the camera ray; and beam, ring and arc at one shared radius. Strategy 31 lets you pick your own pair or trio from ten members.

Every non-temporal dimension is supported. Time is the one dimension left out, because a temporal dimension makes the primitive too hard for the algorithm to reason about and adds much more complexity. A time gate removes one more support dimension, so isolated crossings would need three swept coordinates instead of two, and once the camera leg counts, the level set changes from a sphere around the light to an ellipsoid, with an extra coarea factor, degenerate gradients and several branches.

Earlier photon-primitive implementations, including ours from the photon-primitive research project, supported only a handful of these strategies; stock Tungsten, for one, implements photon points, beams and two plane variants.

A technique created this way rarely covers a whole scene on its own, so the next layer stacks techniques into complete transport.

The representation of a sample changes the problem

Description · at a glance

The central research extension asks how the representation of a sample changes difficult light transport. A narrow caustic may be rarely found by point sampling, while a finite-radius photon estimate spreads energy across the feature. Sweeping selected photon coordinates into geometric support offers another way to reach and resolve those paths.

Photon primitives sample selected variables and sweep the rest into a locus. The camera queries that construction, with density, roots, visibility and supported path classes kept explicit.

Theory / mathematicsPhoton primitives replace selected random choices with a geometric queryThe organizing question is which variables are sampled and which remain integrated.＋

A scattering step in a homogeneous three-dimensional medium can be parameterized by two outgoing direction coordinates and a free-flight distance. A conventional walk samples all three. A photon primitive samples some variables and lets others range continuously, producing a geometric locus of possible positions. The camera ray then queries that locus.

This is a change in how the same transport integral is represented. A sphere can sweep both direction coordinates at a fixed distance; a cone can sweep azimuth and distance at a fixed polar angle; a plane can arise from an emitter coordinate and distance. The geometric construction pays for fewer random choices with a crossing problem, a Jacobian and a support test.

The word analytic must be read at that boundary. A closed-form crossing does not analytically solve every material, visibility event or radiometric factor. It evaluates a constrained family efficiently and leaves the remaining sampled variables and transport terms explicit. The construction becomes valuable when its integrated variables are exactly those that a random walk would otherwise struggle to coordinate.

11 · Mathematical model

Geometry only becomes an estimator after weighting

Description · at a glance

A swept primitive is a change of measure. Its contribution depends on how its parameterized geometry crosses a viewing ray, together with the source, scattering, attenuation and variables still sampled.

The dimensional framework builds on Photon Surfaces (2019) and its follow-ups: any hand-picked set of swept dimensions becomes an estimator once its crossings carry the right Jacobian. The refracted hourglass is the project’s own extension, and the derivations and tests identify its specific assumptions.

Theory / mathematicsGeneralized connections turn sampled constraints into geometric crossingsA geometric hit is only the beginning of a valid contribution.＋

For a two-parameter sheet, the two surface derivatives span an infinitesimal parallelogram. Projecting its oriented area onto the camera direction gives the rate at which sheet parameter area crosses ray arclength. Its absolute value is the geometric denominator of the change of variables. Small values correspond to a grazing or nearly folded crossing.

Every admissible root contributes. A quadratic or quartic can contain several valid crossings as well as algebraic roots outside the primitive’s physical branch. Roots behind the camera, outside the swept interval, on a mirrored cone nappe or occluded along the constructed path must be classified by the same support definition used in the estimator’s density.

The parameterization also determines which probability factors belong in the weight. A sweep uniform in polar angle is not uniform in solid angle. A sweep uniform in cosθ already absorbs the spherical sine factor. Warping a uniform random coordinate into an angle and then multiplying both the warp Jacobian and the same angle PDF counts the conversion twice. The unified notes treat those conversions as explicit entries in a density ledger.

The camera side also determines which variables remain available. A point connection begins at an already sampled medium vertex; a ray connection keeps the camera-distance coordinate free and queries a primitive along that segment. The latter can integrate an event that the former must discover through a sampled distance. Its camera throughput must therefore be taken before consuming that distance law, with the corresponding survival and scattering terms included exactly once. Read the family by what is swept

Description · at a glance

The strategy table has 34 slots, numbered 0–33, including walk baselines, mixed strategies, a rasterized gather route and a user-picked combination. The taxonomy that matters is the geometric support and the paths each estimator represents.

Straight connection families expose how emitter coordinates, directions, and distance can be exchanged for geometry. Refracted constructions add the constraints of a specular chain. Beams, points, rings, and arcs make finite-width support explicit. These distinctions explain both the visual character of low-sample images and the different limiting behavior as work or kernel radius changes.

A dimensional lattice, rather than a list of shapes

At a photon vertex, consider two direction coordinates and one flight distance. Holding distance and sweeping both angles gives a sphere. Holding polar angle and sweeping azimuth and distance gives a cone. Holding azimuth and sweeping polar angle and distance gives a meridian half-plane, called the disk member in the implementation. These are the three complementary ways to sweep two of the three coordinates.

A camera ray and a two-dimensional sheet in three-dimensional space generically meet at isolated points. A curve and a ray generally miss each other, so the one-coordinate ring, arc and beam constructions need finite-width support. The familiar beam is consequently one column of a larger construction. The same count holds for sweeps at any vertex, on the emitter or at the camera aperture; the photon-primitive chapter (Mode 10) gives the general rule.

One-coordinate members have a different solver and a different cost

The ring sweeps azimuth at fixed polar angle and distance; the arc sweeps polar angle at fixed azimuth and distance. Their finite-width circle geometry leads to a stationary-point equation that squares to a quartic. The implementation brackets the roots using the cubic derivative and bisects monotone intervals. All relevant stationary branches must be retained; a nearest-point-only shortcut changes the estimate.

The measured fixed ring/arc/beam mixture suppresses the individual sweeps’ strongest bright outliers, but it draws three light paths per pass. On the recorded unobstructed single-scatter rig it loses to the plain beam on equal time. The documented harder occlusion/emission cases remain future measurements, so robustness in that test is the established benefit.

Why a larger light need not make planes win

There are two different questions: how expensive is one pass, and how many passes are needed for a clean image? The directional trio retains simple sphere, cone and plane queries. The ring and arc solve a more expensive stationary-distance problem even when their width makes the result look smooth. Blur changes support and bias; it does not turn the curved query into a beam query.

A larger emitter helps the planes by expanding their geometric support. The directional mixture is already robust against the members’ complementary tangencies, however, so moving the sampled source across a large emitter need not increase its variance very much. The project’s light-size series exhibits precisely this outcome: the predicted plane/directional crossover was not reached within the tested range. It is a scene-dependent result, not a ranking implied by the shapes alone.

Theory / mathematicsRings and arcs integrate a different variable from a beamTheir shared curve-kernel structure makes the remaining normalization visible.＋

A beam sweeps distance along one direction. A ring instead fixes polar angle and distance and sweeps azimuth; an arc fixes azimuth and distance and sweeps polar angle. They are different one-dimensional integrations of a scattering step, not three decorative ways to draw the same sample. Their radius and axis show exactly which coordinate has been integrated.

A camera ray and a one-dimensional curve do not generically intersect in three dimensions. The documented estimators use a finite transverse support and a curve–ray Jacobian. The derivative of a unit-speed beam differs from the derivative of a ring parameterized by angle: the latter includes its physical radius. Removing that factor would make brightness depend incorrectly on the curve’s scale.

The ring / arc query also exposes a useful numerical pattern. Squaring the stationary-distance condition produces a quartic, but loses a sign condition. The solver isolates candidates on a bounded interval, refines roots and restores the original condition. All admissible stationary branches specified by the estimator must be considered. The broad-scattering ring/arc modes are distinct from the reflection-cone ring variant, whose specular construction and support are different.

Theory / mathematicsA signed-area bug can masquerade as a sampling biasThe arbitrary-plane study separates geometric orientation from probability normalization.＋

Emitter-coordinate planes are useful because a light coordinate and a travel distance can form a simple swept sheet. Generalizing an axis-aligned construction to an arbitrary orientation creates two separate obligations: preserve the geometric map and derive the density under the chosen emitter coordinates. A result that is too bright or too dark does not identify which obligation failed.

The project’s arbitrary-orientation note records a resolved example. The failure came from using an unsigned area in oriented barycentric coordinates. Reversing the emitter winding changed the numerator signs while the denominator stayed positive, rejecting valid light-domain samples. The correction retains the signed area for coordinate reconstruction and uses absolute values only where a nonnegative density or geometric magnitude is required. This is an instructive robustness test for any generalized connection: rotate the scene, reverse an equivalent parameterization and verify that physical radiance remains unchanged. Correlated sampling as an inspection tool

Description · at a glance

Correlated evaluation lets many pixels query one primitive pool, making sheets, arcs and footprints visible in low-sample images. Freezing and resampling the pool separates the construction from a particular draw.

Correct reuse keeps the total-draw normalization and changes spatial covariance. Cached records and conservative gathering investigate when shared construction also reduces execution cost.

The whole roster, independent

Theory / mathematicsA shared photon population changes covariance, not the marginal targetThe denominator is the number of draws, including the ones that miss.＋

In independent sampling, each pixel generates its own primitive population. In correlated sampling, many pixels query the same population. For any fixed pixel, the Monte Carlo sum still estimates the same expectation if the primitive distribution, crossing function and normalization are unchanged. What changes is the relationship between pixels: a single sampled primitive can create a coherent feature across the image.

The normalization is the total number of primitive draws, including samples that miss the pixel or fail to form a contributing primitive. Dividing by the number of crossings conditions on success and biases the estimate. Similarly, drawing until a fixed number of valid primitives is reached requires the acceptance probability to be accounted for. A valid record count and a sample count are different quantities.

Keeping one pool frozen across many camera passes is useful for investigating spatial patterns, but it converges to the conditional result of that finite pool. Fresh light pools are needed to average over light-side uncertainty. Camera jitter and light-pool random streams should also remain distinct, so changing image-space sampling does not accidentally redefine the shared light experiment.

Theory / mathematicsReuse is valuable when it amortizes construction without changing supportThe speed question and the statistical question have separate answers.＋

A shared pool can avoid rebuilding an expensive primitive for every pixel. Rasterization or a spatial bound can then restrict evaluation to rays that might cross it. This is a reordering of the same sum only if the footprint is conservative, every relevant root is retained, and the same ray, primitive weight and total-draw normalization are used.

Curved sheets make that condition nontrivial. A polygonal footprint is not automatically a conservative bound on a curved caustic. Missing even a narrow part of the support can remove energy systematically while making the renderer faster. The cached-record and rasterized routes therefore have explicit supported subsets rather than claiming one acceleration structure works unchanged for every primitive.

At a fixed total primitive-evaluation budget, a larger light pool also means fewer camera-jitter realizations if each pass uses one camera sample. That can worsen edges, depth of field or other camera-side variation even while improving the conditional light estimate. The best pool size depends on which source of variation dominates, as well as construction cost and footprint size.

Path tracer, light tracer and bidirectional path tracer

Description · at a glance

Mode 02 runs every tracer from one GPU compute shader, compiled into feature-specialized kernels. A camera path starts at the pixel and looks for light; a light path starts at the emitter and looks for the camera; bidirectional path tracing (BDPT) grows both and connects them. The light-strategy menu offers seven constructions: Naive (BSDF only), NEE (Next Event Est.), Light Sample Only, MIS (Naive + NEE), Light Tracer (Naive), Light Tracer (NEE) and BDPT (Bidirectional).

The examples show where the strategies work well and where they fail, with an explanation of each case. BDPT runs live, its strategy pyramid updating while the camera walks through the scene. Offline BDPT is common, and so are per-strategy images: Veach’s thesis shows the pyramid, and pbrt-v3’s BDPT integrator can write one image per strategy. An interactive BDPT whose pyramid keeps rendering while you walk and click is another matter: I have never seen one elsewhere, and a search of GitHub found none.

Seven light strategies in one shader

The panel prints the path classes each strategy keeps and loses. Naive BSDF sampling keeps every class, L(D|S|V)*E, but becomes very noisy for small, distant or near-point lights. The NEE option is NEE plus naive emitter hits: shadow connections carry the diffuse transport, and a path that reaches a light directly or through glass and mirrors still counts, so visible lights and caustics stay in the image. Light Sample Only is the pure NEE: it keeps LD*E, ignores every emitter hit, loses directly visible emitters and caustics, and is marked as biased. The two light tracers differ in how they reach the sensor: the naive one must hit a physical aperture, the other connects explicitly to the camera. Light-tracer controls set the splat radius (0 for one pixel, 1 for a 3 × 3 Gaussian, 2 for 5 × 5, up to 4), the light paths per pixel and optional emission from the environment; light selection can be uniform, area-weighted or power-weighted.

This is also the broadest GPU material and medium lane. Its BDPT weights were audited in September 2026; a detail below lists what changed, what was measured and which configurations still fall back.

Live BDPT: the strategy pyramid keeps running while you walk

With the pyramid enabled, the film is split by light-side and eye-side vertex counts while it keeps accumulating. Tiled mode gives each technique its own part of the screen; Shrunken mode shows a miniature of the whole image for each technique. Pure path tracing is described as good for specular chains but not for small or occluded lights; NEE as the workhorse for diffuse transport; a light path that must hit the lens as nearly useless unless the aperture is huge; a light path connected to the camera as excellent for caustics (L S* D E); and an interior connection as diffuse-only, with specular interiors left to merging or photon primitives.

When a strategy cannot reach a path

Each strategy fails exactly where its sampling cannot reach. Four typical failures follow, and the detail below gives the reasons.

  • Light tracer with camera connections: directly visible glass and mirror spheres render black, because an ideal specular vertex cannot be connected to the camera.
  • Naive light tracer: the image is black at a pinhole, because a light path must physically enter the lens; with a very large aperture it fills in slowly, and a wider splat trades noise for blur.
  • Naive path tracer: a random BSDF direction never hits a point light of zero area; NEE samples that light directly.
  • Light Sample Only: visible emitters and the caustic under a glass sphere disappear, because it counts no emitter hits.

Fill only the transport a strategy misses

An option, Fill missing transport with backup estimator, completes NEE, Light Sample Only and both light tracers. It partitions by path support, not by dark pixels: the selected strategy keeps every path it can sample, and a naive or NEE camera path tracer supplies only the missing subset, so no second full image is added. The display can show the combined image, the backup’s share in red, or the backup alone. A pinhole naive light tracer hands the whole camera image to the backup; a finite-aperture one keeps the specular camera paths it can reach.

On the mirror test scene, the completed image’s total radiance was within 0.6% of the camera NEE reference without a medium and within 3.5% with one homogeneous medium, in finite-sample runs. Giving each path exactly one technique is the same principle that photon-primitive stacking applies on a much larger scale.

Theory / mathematicsPT and LT sample the same transport from different endpointsWhere a path starts changes which rare events it must discover by chance.＋

A camera path begins with a pixel and follows scattering events toward possible sources. Next-event estimation adds a deliberate connection from a non-delta vertex to a sampled light. This avoids waiting for a random continuation direction to hit a small emitter, while still requiring the connection to be visible and compatible with the scattering model.

A light path reverses that allocation of effort. It begins where energy is emitted and can discover focused or strongly redirected transport efficiently; getting that energy onto the sensor becomes the difficult event. A light-to-camera connection must include the camera’s measurement response and the appropriate change of measure. A point-like pinhole and a finite lens are not interchangeable endpoints.

The familiar throughput product is only a shorthand for this density ledger. Surface BSDF values, projected cosines, distance conversions, light selection and camera response must be expressed under compatible measures. Delta reflection and refraction are constrained events: an ordinary shadow connection cannot replace a specular chain whose intermediate directions are fixed by geometry.

16 · Mode 03 · CPU photon build, GPU gather

Photon mapping and progressive photon mapping

Description · at a glance

The CPU emits light paths and stores their arrivals in global and caustic photon maps organized by KD-trees; the GPU gathers photons around camera-visible surface points and blends the estimate with the tracer image. In the recorded session, 10,000 light paths produced about 40,000 global and 1,900 caustic photons in 18.6 ms, so the lighting representation is cheap to rebuild and is reused by every pixel.

Photon mapping is fast and biased: a finite gather radius trades fine detail for early, smooth illumination, and while the camera moves the image shows low-frequency blotches rather than fine grain.

Progressive photon mapping: shrink the radius over passes

Progressive photon mapping shrinks the gathering radius while accumulating more photon samples, trading the initial blur for increasing detail. With a suitable shrink schedule, the estimator is consistent: its bias vanishes in the limit. RadianceLab offers the PPM schedule R ← R·√((N+αM)/(N+M)) and a faster direct multiplier R ← αR. The recording uses the direct multiplier at α = 0.7 to make the changing radius easy to see; that shortcut is not the consistent PPM schedule.

Radius, population and reusable work

A larger radius collects more photons and usually makes the image smoother, while spreading illumination across a larger neighborhood. A smaller radius preserves finer structure but exposes the finite sample population. Progressive radius reduction provides a route through that tradeoff. The implementation’s count and radius schedules should be reported explicitly rather than treated as interchangeable with every published progressive photon-mapping variant.

Photon emission and map construction have a cost separate from the gather pass. This makes the mode useful for demonstrating why an apparently cheap camera update can depend on an expensive reusable lighting representation.

Where this implementation stops

The gather happens at the first camera hit that is not a pass-through surface. Hits on metal, glass and mirror materials, and on any surface with roughness below 0.05, receive no photon estimate and render black at full blend; there is no eye-side specular continuation or final gather yet. This is a limit of the implementation, not of photon mapping: Jensen’s method traces camera rays through specular surfaces to a diffuse gather point.

Cached volume photons extend the mode to participating media. For non-overlapping homogeneous sphere or box media with finite area emitters, scattering collisions are baked and gathered with a normalized 3D kernel along camera rays; heterogeneous or emissive interiors are rejected. A homogeneous-slab flux check agreed with an independent estimate to 0.13%.

Theory / mathematicsPhoton gathering exchanges a rare exact connection for a neighborhoodThe kernel radius controls a real bias–variance tradeoff.＋

Conventional photon mapping stores weighted light-path arrivals and estimates incoming flux density near a camera-side surface point. RadianceLab separates global and caustic maps and exposes the KD-tree and gather support. These displays reveal how an estimate is assembled: which photons are in the neighborhood, how densely they cover it, and which path populations reached the map.

A finite gather region smooths the underlying illumination. That can reveal a caustic much earlier than a camera-only estimator, but the smoothing is a property of the estimator, not just a display filter. A surface disk carries an area normalization; a volume ball carries a volume normalization; a beam query has a different transverse support again. Substituting one denominator for another changes energy.

Shrinking the radius improves spatial resolution only if the photon budget also grows enough to populate the smaller neighborhoods. A finite-radius image can be useful and stable while remaining biased. 17 · Modes 04 and 07 · connect or merge

VCM and UPBP (Unifying Points, Beams, and Paths)

Description · at a glance

Connecting two sampled vertices and merging nearby vertices solve different visibility and sampling problems. A connection traces an explicit segment between the eye and light subpaths. A merge accepts a neighborhood around a camera-side interaction and reconstructs illumination from stored light vertices. Combining them is attractive because the useful strategy changes across a scene.

Merging reaches paths that no connection can build. With a pinhole camera and a point light, a caustic seen in a mirror has a specular vertex on both sides of its diffuse vertex, so no pair of sampled vertices can be joined; accepting a light vertex within a small radius estimates that path at the cost of kernel bias. Vertex connection and merging (VCM, Georgiev et al. 2012) combines both kinds of evidence, and Unifying Points, Beams, and Paths (UPBP, Křivánek et al. 2014) extends the combination to participating media.

The shared schematic below keeps the question concrete: how can the same transport be sampled, and what must change when a point connection becomes a finite region? Its first view shows every BDPT split at one path length, the second a VCM surface neighborhood, and the third the volumetric point and beam supports behind UPBP. These are related constructions with different measures, rather than a simple ranking of render quality.

04 · VCM — inspect connections and merges

The CPU VCM lane constructs light paths, stores their vertices in a KD-tree and traces eye paths. Direct illumination, eye–light connections and photon merges can contribute to its film. Connection segments and merge events are retained for visualization, so the effect of radius and local sample support can be examined in the scene.

The current surface gather uses an Epanechnikov kernel and a normal-compatibility check. Connection and merge contributions pass through local photon-density weights with clamps and are then added with a fixed factor of 0.5; the recurrence quantities of the published derivation are stored but not used. This makes the lane an implemented connection-and-merging experiment, not a claim of exact parity with a reference VCM implementation.

The practical comparison is still useful: a small merge radius can reveal sparse local support, while a large radius may wash out a caustic. Showing the connections and merge neighborhoods alongside the film explains that tradeoff more clearly than treating the mode as a black-box quality preset.

Showcase scene 124 places a glass caustic beside ordinary illumination and records connection-plus-merging, connection-only and merging-only films. Its page describes the weights as experimental rather than full path-density MIS.

07 · UPBP — the implemented subset

The published framework unifies volumetric point–point, point–beam and beam–beam estimators with path sampling under one MIS formulation. Mode 07 implements a smaller subset: surface connection and merging and, with the medium enabled, collision volumetric path tracing with NEE plus a surface and volume point gather. When both medium techniques are on, the mode averages the two complete estimates 50/50, which is not the published path-space MIS. With a point light and specular geometry, the mode uses photon gathering for that transport when the gather is enabled, because collision tracing with NEE cannot reach a delta source through glass.

The light walk also records bounded beam segments, and the viewport can draw them. No estimator queries those records: the beam radius changes no pixel and beam–beam merging is inactive. A beam in the overlay therefore illustrates path geometry, not evidence that a beam estimator rendered the film.

The medium path was audited in September 2026, which also removed a double-counted transmittance factor. Absorption through a pass-through sphere gave 0.4505 against a Beer–Lambert value of 0.4493. 18 · Modes 05 and 06 · Markov chains over primary samples

Metropolis light transport: MLT and PSSMLT

Description · at a glance

These modes change the distribution of rendering effort. A Metropolis chain revisits a promising path through correlated proposals instead of drawing every path independently, so bright, hard-to-find transport receives more visits. Neither mode is a neural image denoiser. The intermediate state is part of the demonstration: seed quality, acceptance, spatial coverage and chain traces help explain why a film improves or stalls.

Both modes mutate primary sample values, the random numbers that drive path construction. Path-space MLT with Veach-style geometric mutations is not implemented, and both modes use camera paths on surfaces only.

05 · MLT — bootstrap, mutate, accept and splat

The MLT implementation bootstraps candidate paths, keeps a random-number record for replay, and proposes either a fresh large step or a wrapped Gaussian perturbation. It compares proposed and current luminance, accepts or rejects, and accumulates weighted film splats. Mutation totals, large/small-step counts and acceptance statistics are retained.

The name belongs to the Metropolis light-transport family, but the current mutation code acts on stored random choices. It should not be advertised as a complete collection of Veach-style geometric path-space mutation operators. Its practical controls still expose a real MCMC tradeoff: small proposals exploit a discovered contribution, while large proposals help the chain explore other regions. A concentrated bright patch can indicate inadequate exploration rather than a well-converged result.

06 · PSSMLT — make the random input state explicit

PSSMLT stores the primary sample values that drive path construction and restores them when a proposal is rejected. Bootstrap normalization and mutation-weighted splats connect chain activity to the film. It supplies a separate implementation in which primary-sample bookkeeping and mutation behavior are directly inspectable.

The current code has a dual-splat option that also deposits the rejected portion of the current state. A separate historical “bias fix” applies a small border blur to the displayed image. Those are different operations: one changes estimator accumulation, the other changes display pixels. Neither a smooth border nor a high acceptance rate alone proves global convergence.

Where the chain helps, and where it struggles

Showcase scenes 125 (light through a finite opening) and 126 (two colored islands) compare a large-step probability of 0.3 with all-large steps. Local mutations reveal coherent illuminated surfaces where independent large steps still leave isolated splats; the showcase page reads this as evidence of useful sample reuse, not proof of lower unbiased error.

The usual Metropolis difficulties are visible as well. The image scale rests on the bootstrap estimate; low mutation counts give correlated, blotchy noise; a chain mixes poorly between separated bright regions; effort follows brightness, so dim regions are undersampled; and a camera-path chain cannot invent a sampling strategy for a delta light. Perturbing primary samples also ignores specular constraints, which is the problem manifold exploration addresses in the next chapter.

Theory / mathematicsMetropolis allocates samples through a chainBootstrap normalization, proposal support and state rollback matter as much as the acceptance image.＋

A primary-sample vector contains the random choices used to construct a path. Changing that vector can move a useful path through nearby configurations without rediscovering it from an independent draw. The scalar target commonly derives from the path’s image contribution, so bright, difficult transport attracts more visits. The final estimator must compensate for that allocation; displaying accepted paths alone would not be a radiance estimate.

Large steps maintain access to distant regions of primary-sample space, while small steps explore nearby ones. Acceptance is not a monotonic quality score. Very high acceptance may accompany tiny moves and slow exploration; very low acceptance wastes proposed work. Bootstrap quality, chain coverage and the distribution of contributions explain different failure modes.

Both MLT-labelled modes here perturb random choices. The explicit PSSMLT mode additionally exposes lazy coordinate updates, timestamps and rollback of rejected proposals. These mechanics preserve the state that the next transition assumes. The manifold exploration mode described next changes mutation scales around specular transport, but does not yet solve the specular constraint.

19 · Modes 08 and 09 · finding hard paths

Path guiding and manifold exploration

Description · at a glance

Both modes try to find paths that ordinary sampling rarely reaches, each with its own mechanism. Path guiding learns where useful light arrives and steers later scattering directions toward it. Published manifold exploration starts from a path that already passes through a specular chain and moves it while solving the chain’s constraints; mode 09 approximates this by perturbing primary samples more gently at specular bounces. The first changes a local proposal; the second changes how a Markov chain mutates a complete path.

The shared schematic below isolates the two mechanisms. A normalized directional histogram changes where independent scattering samples go; a one-mirror Newton solve moves a proposed path back onto the specular constraint. Both expose the quantities needed to reason about the method, while keeping the native implementation boundaries visible.

Each mechanism has blind spots. A guide needs training passes, can overfit early noisy evidence, cannot steer through a delta BSDF and cannot create support for a path that has zero sampling probability, such as a point light seen through glass. Manifold exploration needs a seed path on the right specular chain, and a constraint solve can fail or converge to a different branch.

08 · Path guiding — a spatial field of directional histograms

Initial training passes collect path contributions into a regular 3D grid. Each cell stores a 16 × 16 angular histogram. Later diffuse samples can consult that field, controlled by a guiding-strength setting, and the system retains arrows and guided-sample records for visualization. The field can be inspected as a learned proposal distribution rather than inferred only from a less noisy image.

Spatial resolution, from 8 to 32 cells per axis, controls how locally the scene is represented; the directional bin count is fixed at 16 × 16, a restriction documented after a decoding bug was found. Training cost, memory and stale information after a scene change are practical tradeoffs. This implementation is a grid-based experiment, not an adaptive SD-tree or neural guiding system.

Its probability bookkeeping has a known gap: the guided branch divides by the mixture density, but the cosine branch does not evaluate the same mixture. Current images, including showcase scene 127 (a doorway at guiding strength 0.5 versus 0), therefore demonstrate learning behavior and cannot support an unbiased variance-reduction claim until that is repaired.

09 · Manifold exploration — a prototype without the Newton solve

Manifold exploration (Jakob and Marschner 2012) is a Metropolis mutation that keeps specular chains valid by solving their constraints with local Newton steps. Mode 09 is a prototype of that idea built on primary-sample-space Metropolis: random numbers consumed at a near-specular bounce are perturbed at one tenth of the usual scale, so specular chains survive a mutation more often. The panel describes this as helping specular–diffuse–specular paths; no measurement of that effect is recorded. Large steps still replace the proposal state, and mutation and specular-chain records are kept for visualization within the same interactive framework.

The Newton constraint solve is not built yet: its iteration and step-size controls are inactive, and the reported Newton step count is zero. The defensible demonstration is therefore how a specular-aware proposal changes chain behavior. A complete manifold walk would additionally solve the constraint system and account for the induced proposal measure. Showcase scene 128, a relay through two glass objects, is kept as a development fixture for that step.

Reading the two schematics

The guiding view is a one-angle slice with 64 bins and a fixed two-lobe integrand. The training slider sharpens the learned guide, the mixture slider moves between pure BSDF sampling and pure guiding, and the readout gives the exact variance of this discrete model while the integral stays fixed, because every sample is divided by the full mixture probability. The manifold view fixes a light and a mirror: dragging the sensor breaks the old reflection path, each Newton step moves the mirror vertex along the tangent of the constraint, and the readouts report the step count, the remaining constraint residual and the distance to the exact reflected-sensor solution. Both views explain the mechanism; neither renders a native film.

Different forms of adaptation

MLT and PSSMLT adapt where a correlated chain spends its effort. Path guiding builds a reusable directional sampling field. Photon-primitive construction changes which dimensions are integrated geometrically. These are related tools for difficult transport, but they are not synonyms or a simple ranking of increasing quality.

Theory / mathematicsGuiding changes a local proposal rather than following a Markov chainDirectional histograms are useful only when their probability is included in the estimator.＋

The path-guiding experiment accumulates directional information in a uniform spatial grid. Its directional bins learn where useful incident transport has arrived, providing a proposal for future scattering directions. This attacks a different problem from Metropolis: it changes how a new path is sampled at a vertex, rather than deciding whether to accept a mutation of an existing complete path.

A mixture with the BSDF proposal preserves a route to directions that the guide has not yet observed. The mixture probability must be evaluated for the actual sampled direction, regardless of which component generated it. Otherwise the guide changes brightness as well as variance. Empty cells, sparse directional bins and scene invalidation are therefore part of the sampling contract.

The histogram display makes this an interpretable learning system. A narrow learned lobe is useful if it tracks important incoming radiance, but can also expose overconcentration on noisy early evidence. Compare the guide, the sampled directions and the film rather than equating a visually sharp histogram with a better estimator. No neural denoiser is involved in this mode.

21 · Variance and consistency

Why complementary estimators matter

Description · at a glance

Complementary constructions can control one another’s difficult regions through multiple importance sampling. The comparison requires compatible contributions, a common measure and the same physical support.

Finite-radius members retain a smoothing discrepancy. Radius ladders, progressive shrinkage and explicit guard settings make that bias–variance tradeoff part of the experiment.

A method name is not a guarantee

Report the strategy, supported path class, radius and shrink schedule together. A finite-radius mixture, an analytic component, and an appearance-oriented composite establish different kinds of results.

Theory / mathematicsMIS compares densities of the same eventThe useful question is not which estimator is globally best, but which has credible support here.＋

Multiple importance sampling combines alternative ways of producing one contribution. Their densities must describe the same event under a common measure. A solid-angle PDF, a surface-area PDF and a camera-ray arclength PDF cannot be added directly. Geometry terms, source selection and branch multiplicity convert them before weighting.

Support is as important as density magnitude. If a technique cannot generate a path—because of a delta interface, a light type, a camera leg or a restricted sheet branch—its competing density is zero for that event. An attractive geometric proximity does not create support. Conversely, a valid branch must not disappear merely because one solver’s guard differs from the guard used in cross-evaluation.

For compatible unbiased estimators of the same target, weights forming a partition of unity can preserve expectation. Finite-radius photon estimates introduce another qualification: they approximate a smoothed target, and shrinking-radius consistency must be discussed separately. This is why the brochure distinguishes complete common-measure reasoning from the current heuristic combination in some conventional CPU modes.

Optional progressive radius shrinkage exposes the smoothing tradeoff over time, but practical analytic members still have root tolerances, guards and truncation. Guard-on and guard-off energy should be reported separately; suppressing a difficult contribution can change the target rather than merely reduce variance.

Theory / mathematicsComplementary support can suppress a fold without deleting itBeam–hourglass mixing is a local density comparison, with unrepresented paths left to the beam.＋

On a compatible transmitted branch, a beam-based construction and an hourglass construction can contain the same full spatial Jacobian in their deposited densities. Cancelling that shared factor simplifies their relative weights. The resulting numerators still retain the sampling laws, interface transmissions, kernel support and hourglass sheet Jacobian required by their respective constructions.

Near a fold, the hourglass contribution contains a small sheet Jacobian in its denominator. Its local MIS numerator also decreases with that Jacobian, allowing a supported beam alternative to take more responsibility there. This addresses a region of poor conditioning through another estimator rather than discarding large values with an arbitrary display clamp.

That cancellation is conditional on the shared branch. A beam can represent paths with internal reflection or other relays that the two-interface hourglass does not support; in those regions it cannot be downweighted by an imaginary hourglass competitor. The optional point-density discussion adds a different radius order, but does not imply that every documented mixed strategy enables every member simultaneously.

22 · Anatomy of variance

Separate missed paths from unstable weights

Description · at a glance

Three questions diagnose a noisy estimate: how often does it contribute, how singular is its crossing weight, and how well does sampling track the remaining radiometric factors?

Acceptance rates, conditional weights and contribution tails separate those causes. They also explain why a finite sample variance can remain unstable near a fold even when the mean is meaningful.

Theory / mathematicsRare hits and large weights cause different kinds of noiseThe variance study separates acceptance probability from the tail of accepted contributions.＋

Many primitive samples contribute exactly zero because their sheet misses the camera ray, the constructed path fails a domain check or a light-side closure misses the emitter. Among successful samples, contribution weights can still span a large range. Those mechanisms call for different interventions: a better proposal improves acceptance, while a different parameterization or complementary estimator can improve the accepted-weight distribution.

A generic fold offers a sharp example of the second mechanism. As two crossings coalesce, the Jacobian can decrease like the square root of distance to the fold. Its reciprocal remains integrable in the first moment but can have a logarithmically divergent second moment under a regular sampling density. A finite observed sample variance may then keep changing as rarer events appear, rather than settling into a reliable error bar.

That local model is not a blanket statement that every image or pixel has infinite variance. Scene visibility, phase functions, sampled domains and the location of the fold relative to the camera matter. 23 · One primitive, three different questions

Geometry, variance, and rendering cost

Description · at a glance

A fully shared sphere pool makes complete glowing shells visible. That does not automatically make the renderer a rasterizer: the current CPU sphere, disk, cone and direction-trio routes reproduce the pool from its seeds in every pixel. 27 · Spectral rendering and dispersion

Split white light: spectral rendering and dispersion

Description · at a glance

One switch turns the GPU path tracer spectral. Each camera path, and each light path, samples one wavelength uniformly between 380 and 780 nm, and CIE 1931 color matching converts the result back to RGB. The conversion is normalized so a flat, equal-energy spectrum keeps the luminance of RGB mode. Spectral mode is a separately compiled shader variant; the log shows the path-trace kernel recompiling when the switch is flipped.

Surfaces and light sources become spectra. Each material holds up to eight Gaussian lobes over the visible range, edited on a wavelength canvas that replaces the color picker. A color that was never edited becomes up to three narrow lobes at 467, 532 and 630 nm, and a Smoothing control widens every lobe. A light source takes its emission spectrum from the same curve, scaled by its strength.

Glass disperses through a Cauchy index anchored at the sodium D line, so the IOR slider keeps its usual meaning and a Dispersion slider sets how strongly the index changes with wavelength (1 is close to real glass, larger values exaggerate it). RGB mode disperses too, by sampling one color channel at a time. The CPU photon-primitive estimators use the same index at the three channel wavelengths, so the analytical hourglass caustic picks up colored fringes as well.

Surface reflectance, glass transmission and emission are read from each material's Gaussian-lobe curve at that wavelength and multiplied into the path as a gray factor.

Smoothing widens every lobe for a broader spectrum.

Theory / mathematicsIn a medium, survival is not another scattering densityThe same exponential appears in different places depending on what was sampled.＋

A homogeneous medium separates extinction, scattering and absorption. Extinction determines the probability of reaching a distance without an event; scattering determines the amount of that removed radiance redirected into another direction. Sampling an exponential distance and evaluating a fixed-distance connection are two different estimators, even when both contain the same transmittance function.

For a sampled free-flight event, the distance PDF can cancel the corresponding attenuation factor in the throughput ratio. For a geometric primitive that sweeps a distance instead of sampling it, attenuation remains in the integrand under the swept measure. A boundary reached before scattering is a survival event with probability mass, not a sampled medium vertex with the same differential density as an interior collision.

This bookkeeping becomes especially important around refractive chains. Camera-side transmittance, medium distance before the sphere, and the outgoing swept segment may enter through different sampled or deterministic operations. Duplicating a survival factor darkens a valid estimator; dropping one brightens it. The unified notes make this a per-segment ledger rather than relying on a single generic “throughput” multiplier.

Theory / mathematicsAn emission profile and a sampling distribution are different objectsThe emitter-cosine investigation isolates a subtle source of cross-integrator brightness errors.＋

Radiance is power per projected area per solid angle. Illumination can encode a measurement

Description · at a glance

The renderer can act as an optical bench. Sampled projectors, path-length gates and four-bucket time of flight turn the scene into a controlled measurement setup, and rendered observations flow into spectra, phase recovery and depth diagnostics with the intermediate stages on screen. An off-axis holography pipeline is here as well; holography itself has its own projects, Holography & depth reconstruction and Aperture, linked below.

Rendered observations and synthetic-depth controls remain separate. Ground-truth-guided unwrapping is labeled, and the “shot noise” control is an illustrative uniform perturbation rather than a photon-counting noise model.

Theory / mathematicsFour intensity measurements recover a phase, not automatically a unique depthModulation, wrapping and multipath give the optical experiment its actual inference problem.＋

The CW time-of-flight experiment modulates illumination and measures four demodulation phases. Their differences cancel the phase-independent background and isolate two quadratures. The resulting angle is periodic, so one modulation frequency measures path length only modulo its wavelength. A useful decoder must distinguish a wrapped phase measurement from an unwrapped geometric distance.

For a collocated source and sensor with a single direct round trip, path length is twice the surface distance. A non-collocated setup, indirect bounce or scattering path has a different geometric interpretation. Multiple arrivals combine as phasors before the angle is recovered; the measured phase is generally neither the shortest path nor an intensity-weighted arithmetic average of depths.

RadianceLab separates rendered modulation buckets from synthetic-depth tests of the decoder. The latter are useful controls because the input geometry is known. Ground-truth-guided selection of an integer wrap is also a useful validation instrument, but it does not demonstrate blind unwrapping. Spatial projector and sensor phase ramps must be accounted for before interpreting the recovered phase as distance.

Theory / mathematicsPhase unwrapping is a weighted reconstruction problem with a gauge freedomThe linear solver, mask and residual reveal what a smooth recovered phase actually means.＋

Wrapped phase gives a principal angle at each pixel. Neighboring differences can be reduced into that same principal interval, but those local differences need not integrate into one globally consistent surface after noise, masking or discontinuities. Weighted least squares reconstructs a phase field whose gradients fit the observed wrapped differences as closely as possible.

The normal equations form a weighted graph Laplacian. Adding a constant to every recovered phase leaves the objective unchanged, so a gauge must be fixed or the global offset removed before comparing against ground truth. A small reported error is otherwise sensitive to an arbitrary constant rather than reconstruction quality.

The implementation uses a Poisson-FFT preconditioner inside a weighted solve. That preconditioner approximates the operator; it is not the inverse of every spatially varying weight field. Strong amplitude variation can make the system harder to condition. Uniform weighting over valid neighbors can improve the match to the preconditioner, but gives weak, noisy regions more influence. The residual and amplitude coverage make that tradeoff inspectable.

Once the composition is right, Take a shot (F12, or Render › Take a high-quality shot) renders a finite exposure of the current view at a chosen output size and pass budget, with the current renderer at higher quality or CPU VCM as a comparison estimator, and then restores the preview settings.

An exposure can hold the camera still or move it through a frozen scene: pan, dolly, orbit around a subject or dolly zoom. Each camera position receives the full pass budget, and linear radiance is averaged before a single tone map; the same movement can also be written as a time-lapse image sequence.

Each shot is saved in its own timestamped folder: a PNG of the finished viewport before selection outlines, a scene-linear HDR and PFM film where the renderer exposes one (GPU tracing, VCM, UPBP, path guiding and photon primitives), and a JSON record of sample budget, renderer, camera and frozen scene time.

  • Compose: walk in first person, select geometry and place lights or participating media.
  • Author: connect typed nodes, inspect intermediate previews, and apply the material or medium graph to the scene.
  • Explore: switch the transport route and inspect photons, paths, connection strategies, mutation activity or guiding fields.
  • Frame: set focus, aperture shape and tilt-shift, and use dolly zoom to change perspective.
  • Shoot: take a shot at the chosen size and pass budget, still or with camera motion.
  • Preserve: keep the PNG, linear HDR/PFM and JSON record; use comparison exports and linear film dumps for numerical studies.
ExplanationThe scene, camera and actual image producer define the experimentInteraction settings are part of the record, not just presentation preferences.＋

A first visit can start with a material or lighting showcase, object selection and a camera move. A transport study benefits from a simpler scene where path classes are recognizable. The one-ball hourglass setup isolates the refracted construction before a larger caustic room introduces additional visibility and support questions.

A repeatable session retains camera pose, exposure, source power, medium coefficients, material support and integrator settings. The displayed image must be attributed to the active producer rather than the name last selected in a menu.

Take a shot is the dedicated shutter. While it renders, the resolution scale is forced to one, the photon-primitive film switches to full resolution, node and volume animation are frozen and temporal blur is off; the preview settings return when it finishes or is cancelled. A native validation run recorded a still, a long exposure and a three-frame sequence, plus raster and VCM captures, with finite HDR radiance where the film is linear. Comparison-report exports and photon-primitive linear films remain the routes for numerical studies.

Unsupported combinations are refused rather than silently changed: tilt-shift needs a naive, NEE or MIS eye-ray strategy, the CPU estimators need a pinhole, and camera motion needs a linear film. Changing the scene, camera type or renderer during a capture cancels it and restores the preview.

The Primitive Explorer adds a different level of inspection: it displays the sampled construction, camera crossings and Jacobian-related quantities. Geometry views are especially useful when an image error could arise from either a bad weight or a bad hit test.

The in-process validator probes selected quantities, schematics identify sampled and swept coordinates, and the ray tree follows transport through the scene. The important discipline is to name what a view proves. A stored beam is evidence of beam generation, not necessarily beam gathering. A weight visualization shows evaluated contributions, not every point in the mathematical density. A screenshot after tone mapping is evidence of appearance, not a linear energy measurement. These limits make the diagnostics more useful because they suggest the next experiment rather than inviting an unsupported conclusion.

Seeing is where checking starts, not where it ends. The project’s own rule is that these views decide where to look, while reference films and ratios decide whether a number is right. In a deliberate wrong-divisor experiment, an image that looked correct at every sample count converged to about 4.45 times the reference brightness; only a reference film and a median ratio exposed it.

35 · A smaller authoring feature

Bake a projected image into a material

Description · at a glance

An imported image or clean-frame capture can be baked into a mesh’s texture coordinates, so it becomes part of the material and stays attached to the surface when the view changes. Before baking, the image can be projected through a stored camera pose and previewed live on surfaces.

UV baking changes material appearance; the optical bench’s projector changes illumination. Keeping those routes distinct makes their assumptions and capabilities clear.

How the bake is made

Bake to UV rasterizes the projected image into each supported mesh’s UV texture and supplies it to the tracer as albedo. Besides aspect fitting, vertical orientation and edge fade, it has a bake-resolution control, explicit Re-bake and a side-selection rule: the side facing the projector, the positive-normal side, or both. The other five routes do not bake: Reproject works on the displayed image, Project applies the image at surface hits with an unlit or re-lit albedo, Forward uses the raster surface route, and Light cone and Light plane add stylized projected illumination rather than the structured-light lamp’s shadow-tested estimator.

The tracer applies an explicit plane-basis correction so its analytic plane UV convention matches the baked mesh, and keeps its existing material fill in uncovered UV gaps. The result still depends on usable mesh UVs and projection coverage, and the bake-size slider does not guarantee the size every consumer uses: the tracer has its own material-array resolution.

A typical baking sequence

  • Capture or import an image with its camera pose.
  • Preview it with Project, then Bake to UV and Re-bake.
  • Commit, move the camera or a light: the image stays on the surface as albedo.
ExplanationBaking, projection and projector illumination answer different questionsThe same image can be a display effect, a surface attribute or a sampled light source.＋

The camera node supplies a projection pose and the texture node supplies image values. A screen-space use places those values in the image; a live surface projection evaluates them through the chosen camera; Bake to UV transfers the result into the mesh’s texture coordinates. After the bake, the material is attached to the surface rather than to the viewing camera.

That is an authoring operation, not inverse reflectance recovery. A photograph can already contain shadows, highlights and exposure decisions. Baking it into albedo preserves those image features, and subsequent lighting can illuminate them again. The useful demonstration is the transformation between coordinate systems and stages, with that assumption stated.

The optical experiment’s projector is different: it enters the transport estimator as sampled illumination and therefore participates in light visibility, scattering and path-length modulation. Comparing the two workflows helps separate “an image that looks projected” from an image whose values were actually transported through the scene.

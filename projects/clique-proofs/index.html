Clique Proofs

An educational proof atlas, still growing: why do Prim and Kruskal both work, and why can compactness have such different definitions? Follow direct arguments between equivalent viewpoints and inspect the assumptions each route needs.

The ideaTwo mathematical statements can describe the same underlying property while suggesting very different proofs. This atlas connects equivalent viewpoints through direct arguments and makes their required assumptions explicit.

The idea behind it

I find it intriguing when apparently unrelated definitions describe exactly the same structure. I wanted direct routes between those viewpoints, where understanding why Prim and Kruskal both work or how compactness changes its appearance reveals more than the equivalence alone.

Working intuition A direct argument can reveal structure that the statement of equivalence leaves hidden.

Deliberate scope This educational atlas is a work in progress. Its purpose is to connect established results through explicit proofs and assumptions; some clusters are more developed than others, and the graph is not a machine-checked proof library.

Write the direct argument, then compare the route

This is a work-in-progress learning atlas. The aim is to explain known connections through direct arguments and visual navigation; the novelty sought is in the exposition and choice of route, rather than a claim of new theorems.

The starting point is a familiar implementation question: two algorithms behave differently, yet both are correct. The next step is to inspect their shared invariants and the direct proof that connects those viewpoints. Compactness supplies a different setting for the same investigation: apparently unrelated definitions can characterize one property, but the assumptions and the route between them matter.

A textbook can establish equivalence with a short chain. Clique Proofs aims to fill the direct arguments between viewpoints: reaching C from A through B and proving A → C directly can expose different constructions, invariants or certificates. The extra edges carry the substance of the project.

The graph is a way into the argument. Select a statement or relation, inspect the proof and its assumptions, then compare another route through the same cluster. Density is useful when the selected edge remains readable and its reasoning can be examined.

Start with a focused cluster

The focused edition selects fourteen proof webs, three implication ladders and a counterexample collection. MST connects cuts, cycles and exchange arguments; the compactness cluster compares covers and other witnesses, with hypotheses stated on each relation; Fourier operators connect spatial and frequency viewpoints. For example, sequential compactness and compactness agree in metric spaces, but that equivalence cannot simply be asserted for every topological space.

The full archive retains wider subject roadmaps and cross-field bridges. Both editions share the same entry explanation, including a small diagram distinguishing a direct argument from a chain of implications.

Make the limits of an equivalence visible

One-way implications, scoped equivalences, open questions and counterexamples have distinct meanings. The assumptions on an edge matter as much as its endpoints; a visually dense graph is not evidence that every nearby concept is interchangeable.

Spectral, Fourier, geometric and algorithmic examples make the practice relevant to engineering: a different formulation may suggest a representation, a certificate of correctness or a more useful algorithm. The atlas demonstrates that habit through authored explanations.

Current scope

  • Work in progress: clusters and proof edges have different levels of detail. A visible relation is an invitation to inspect its proof and hypotheses, not a claim that the full archive is complete or formally verified.
  • The atlas is authored mathematical exposition, not automated theorem proving. Relations and proof text are curated by hand.
  • The aim of developing direct proof edges does not mean every possible edge in every topic has a completed direct proof. Open, one-way and conditional relationships retain their own labels.

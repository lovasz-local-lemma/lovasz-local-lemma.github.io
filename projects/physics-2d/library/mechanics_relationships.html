Frame Loop

Why the ordering matters

The simulation is field-mediated. Particles do not only collide with each other; they write state into grids, the grids solve global effects, then particles read those fields back.

StageCommon failure when misordered
Raster before solveStale charge or magnetization makes forces lag and feel like a filter.
Scene-only magnetic solvePrevents ferro from sustaining its own field after the source is gone.
Post force passesKeeps SSBO binding pressure low and isolates chain/Coulomb behavior.
G2P coloring lastDebug views see the final force, thermal, and field state for that frame.

Mechanics Atlas

MPM materials

Elastic, plastic, brittle, thermal, charged, ferro, bio, and foot-demo particles share P2G/G2P plumbing with material-specific stress branches.

SPH fluids

Neighbor-density fluids with pressure, viscosity, XSPH smoothing, CSF surface tension, thermal states, and MPM contact.

Eulerian air

MAC-grid velocity, temperature, smoke, vapor, airtight pressure, blast, reaction diffusion, and continuous automata.

SDF world

Kinematic colliders with friction, thermal diffusion, and magnetic source presets. Mostly one-way collision into particles.

Magnetics

SDF/cursor/particle magnetization writes M, Poisson solve gives H, and MPM reads Kelvin force plus surface Maxwell stress.

Electrostatics

Charge rasterization, Poisson solve, and post-MPM Coulomb acceleration for positive/negative ion materials.

Thermal effects

Particle heat softens, burns, melts, boils, vents vapor, pressurizes cavities, changes magnetic phases, and drives regrowth.

Visualization

Debug views expose velocity, stress, temperature, magnetic fields, charges, bio fields, automata drive, and authored layer colors.

Ferrofluid Lens

What changed in this checkpoint
  1. Packed magnetic cells add pressure.
  2. Thin connected sheets get mild bridge tension.
  3. Cursor-follow lift keeps more source force.
  4. Spike stress is more surface-weighted.

What should look physical

Large volumes should climb when the source creates a broad upward gradient. The climb should be a column or wick, not a rigid hook. Stronger fields should increase surface corrugation and spike height before they merely fling isolated grains.

What is still approximate

The ferro free surface is not a full Rosensweig instability solve. It is a stack of practical terms: weakly compressible Tait pressure, Kelvin body force, field-aligned stress, demag pressure, occupancy capillarity, and pseudo-2.5D thickness.

Reliability of thickness

Thickness is reliable as an art-directable closure for a 2D slice. It helps with tunnels, columns, and fluffy/porous materials. It is not a conserved hidden-volume model, so extreme values can hide real separation or make sheets feel too sticky.

Thermal, Explosion, and Regrowth Lens

Thermal chain

Particles carry temperature and material-specific thermal coupling. Hot particles inject heat, vapor, smoke, and sometimes combustion into air. Air temperature then cools or heats particles on the next G2P pass.

Explosion chain

Combustion and sealed-charge materials feed vapor, heat, airtight pressure, and blast impulses. The cavity mask decides whether gas can vent or builds pressure into a local explosion.

Regrowth chain

Ash now acts mostly as scaffold/support. Live regrowth seeds the bio and automata fields; ash alone no longer seeds a strong dark automata front. Recovery accelerates color restoration.

Page relationship to the in-app graph

The pipeline graph view is excellent for frame-order debugging. This page is meant to answer a different question: when a material does something surprising, which fields and couplings could be responsible?

Use the status report for implementation truth, this page for mental model, and the in-app views for live evidence. The useful workflow is: pick the symptom, identify the field path, toggle one force family, then compare against the expected visual from the diagrams.

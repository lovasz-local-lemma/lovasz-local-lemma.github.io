How this experiment works

Density, heat and velocity survive between frames in a 3D simulation. Compute passes advect the field, add buoyancy and solve a pressure correction. The same density scatters the flashlight and attenuates its surface light, refracted beams and caustics. Freeze the flow, then move the light to inspect one volume.

Rive’s current GPU Canvas API has no compute dispatch. This implementation runs beside the ordinary Rive interface. A different solver could use render passes; smoke itself is not an exclusive effect. The coarse grid, seeded plumes and beam-driven force are a visual fluid approximation, not radiation-pressure physics or a multiple-scattering solver.

Shape the torch · power, direction and colour

Brightness changes emitted power; the gallery's ambient light stays. Yaw and tilt offset the continuous Rive aim. Disk and star sample a real emitting area perpendicular to the torch axis, at the same total power. RGB separation changes the glass index for red, green and blue rays.

Area emitters soften and reshape the caustic; they are not a star-shaped screen mask. Five source samples approximate direct surface light; direct fog uses the centre of the emitter. RGB is a three-band dispersion study, not a spectrum. Larger RGB separation needs more stationary batches to smooth the colour samples.

02 / THE WORK MOVES

…to a choice of evaluation.

The baseline uses triangle rasterization and a depth buffer. Within the flashlight, analytical intersections add reflected and refracted views. Emitted rays first intersect the scene, refract through the glass and deposit finite photon footprints on the first receiver they reach. The same paths form a soft volumetric caustic. Occluded direct fog and the refracted beams share the optical time window. Drift, absorption and emission tints are deliberately artistic medium models, inspired by the transport studies in RadianceLab and LTC.

When still, the receiver and refracted beam buffers average independent emitted populations before tone mapping. Sample totals keep growing without a four-batch cap. Moving geometry or an animated medium invalidates the old image and uses a low-cost preview. A nearby time-gate sweep can keep a short exponential history; it deliberately trails the current ruler slightly. Holding still starts a fresh arithmetic mean at the exact gate. Fixed spatial bandwidth and finite beam width remain biased; this is progressive averaging, not a shrinking-kernel PPM estimator.

Gyroid membranes concentrate fog into thin periodic sheets; dense pockets alternate with almost empty channels. Scale and contrast reshape that density. Short density quadrature affects attenuation as well as scattering. Like the procedural-medium studies in RadianceLab, this makes the field itself visible, but this compact browser route uses a bounded approximation rather than full heterogeneous transport.

The zebra library keeps the original oxide/copper pair. Added looks change roughness and metal response; the glass bands trace a refracted background, the steel pair uses an anisotropic highlight, and relief perturbs the normal. Laser foil is an artistic angle-dependent interference colour, not a spectral diffraction solver. This remains a deliberately biased optical study. It is not a complete path tracer, a converged global-illumination estimate or a multiple-scattering volume solver.

03 / THE RIVE CONNECTION

The instrument belongs to Rive.

The reticle, optical-time ruler, labels and three primary sliders are actual Rive vector content. The scene editor, setup list and Living fog switches are host controls. They share values with the GPU renderer, so the composition remains interactive across the boundary.

Here, a RIVX Suite carries the ordinary .riv interface and its external WebGPU player. The companion GPU Canvas authoring explores packaging the optical render passes inside a Rive document; its browser release has a separate compile/signing step. Living fog uses compute passes in the external player. These material, medium and handle variants belong to the browser Suite; they have not been copied into that private companion document.

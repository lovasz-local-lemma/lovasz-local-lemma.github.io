Retained data, fitting method and limits
What was actually retained

A bake is a choice of dependencies.

The image stores the final view. The surface bake stores illumination before multiplication by surface color: two 128 × 128 sphere charts and a 256 × 256 floor chart. Turning off editable color also locks the captured color parameter. These are real RGBA8 textures, with scalar irradiance encoded over [0, 4].

The retained scene keeps two spheres, one floor, an orbiting point light and Lambertian material colors. Illumination includes inverse-square falloff, hard sphere visibility and a constant ambient term. There is no specular, glass, indirect-light transport or native RIVX file parser in this controlled study.

What the derivative means

Sensitivity is local. Fitting needs evidence.

The middle image evaluates [Y(θ + h) − Y(θ − h)] / 2h before tone mapping. Cyan decreases and orange increases; brightness uses a fixed compressive display scale. A hard shadow boundary is discontinuous, so its finite-difference band depends on the chosen step. This is a numerical sensitivity view, not an analytic visibility derivative.

The solver uses damped Gauss–Newton steps, finite-difference Jacobians and backtracking on sampled, visible sphere pixels. The material study first surveys light angles and fits power from the residuals to find a useful starting basin. Camera, geometry, discrete material family and texture frequencies remain known. Roughness, bump amplitude, colour mix and a texture contrast coefficient can join angle and power as unknowns. Each accepted endpoint lowers the objective; the smooth motion between endpoints is presentation, not an extra solver step. The floor is excluded from the loss.

This connects the material studies in LTC Light Studio and RadianceLab with the image-matching perspective of Inverse Render Lab: which lighting produced this image? The focused scene uses the same compact GGX response on CPU and GPU. It is a browser adaptation, not a port of either full renderer.

For a whole texture, one view is not enough to separate reflectance, lighting, normals and exposure reliably. A useful next step is a low-dimensional texture basis plus regularization and additional views or illuminations. This study fits one contrast coefficient; it does not learn every texel or infer unknown geometry.

The solver follows the same residual-vector and damped Gauss–Newton approach investigated in Inverse Render Lab. The small GGX CPU/GLSL pair is implemented here as a controlled adaptation; this is not the research renderer or its full visibility derivative.

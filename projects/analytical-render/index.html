Analytical Render · LTC

Linearly Transformed Cosines approximate a complex BRDF lobe with a transformed cosine, turning direct lighting from polygonal area lights—especially quads—into closed-form edge integrals. Compare that deterministic result against Monte Carlo in a material garage while the fit and geometry stay inspectable.

The ideaA large light source illuminates a surface from many directions at once. Linearly Transformed Cosines approximate the material's directional response so direct lighting from a polygon can be calculated with a compact geometric integral.

The idea behind it

An area light seems to ask for many samples, yet a fitted change of coordinates can turn the problem into a compact boundary calculation. The appeal is being able to see both the geometric shortcut and exactly where its approximation departs from the sampled reference.

An extended light without a cloud of samples

Soft area lighting normally integrates a response over many incident directions. For a uniform polygonal emitter, the projected solid angle gives an exact diffuse building block under the stated visibility assumptions. LTC extends that geometric route to a fitted glossy distribution.

What the comparison measures

The garage compares deterministic fitted GGX LTC and Monte Carlo direct lighting for the same opaque material model. It is not a full path-tracing comparison. The glass, thin-film and numerical medium terms are shared scene effects; the cube’s single scattering is not analytically integrated by LTC.

JS and WASM expose compatible diffuse studies. The garage’s fitted materials and additional scene effects use the WebGPU implementation, so changing the backend also selects a supported experiment.

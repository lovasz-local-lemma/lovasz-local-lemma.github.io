The square-root tail describes nondegenerate tangency at fixed δ > 0. At δ = 0, shrinking t instead gives J = t², a different limit. Misses are zero; an exactly tangent ray is a measure-zero boundary where the ideal pointwise shell density diverges. A finite pixel footprint and a pointwise cutoff are different regularizations.

A complementary density covers the troublesome region.

Integrate g(x) = 1 over [0,1]. Draw one sample from an equal mixture q = ½(p₁ + p₂) and return 1/q(x). A uniform partner covers the endpoint; a second density with the same zero leaves it exposed.

Wflat = 1 / pchosen(x); Wbalance = 1 / q(x)

These are two unbiased one-sample estimators on a common dx measure. Flat weighting averages independently unbiased strategy estimates; balance uses the mixture denominator. With a uniform partner q ≥ ½, balance weights are bounded by 2, but flat weighting still has an infinite second moment for k ≥ 1. With p₂ = p₁, both retain that endpoint divergence. This is not an unweighted sum of two stratified estimates, and MIS is not a universal cure when all densities share the same bad region.

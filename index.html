Flagships

These six projects are the ones I'm most proud of: each combines technical depth, refinement and innovation in its design. RadianceLab is an interactive CPU/GPU rendering system with ten rendering modes, including methods from my own research paper and unpublished follow-ups. RIVX explores how rendered 3D scenes can become Rive animation, alongside extended drawing, editing and live GPU computation. Modal2D is a physically based audio simulator and musical instrument designer that lets you create music by simulating physical interactions, with no recorded samples needed. Crucible2.5D explores coupled materials and their changing surfaces; Symbolic Unfolded makes symbolic reasoning inspectable; Fortune’s Loom exposes the geometry, data structures and difficult cases of a sweep-line algorithm.

Modal2D

Sound

Create a fully physically based virtual musical instrument, starting with a shape, its mass and stiffness. FEM gives its vibration modes; a strike, bow or rub excites those modes to reconstruct the sound. Each shape has its own sound profile. Turn it into an instrument through careful manual tuning or an algorithm that adjusts the geometry to reach musical notes, then assemble the instruments into choirs.

Fun factNot one recorded sample: every note, even a choir playing a score, comes from the shapes' own vibration modes.

Crucible2.5D

Physics

A comprehensive 2D physical simulation laboratory coupling MPM, SPH and Eulerian fluids, with tearing, heating, anisotropic material structure, baking, explosions, magnetism and living materials that can grow. It's called 2.5D because I added approximate depth support to keep porous materials connected: a tunnel through a flat 2D slice can split it in two, while material outside that slice can still hold it together.

Symbolic Unfolded

Math

A hand-built Ruby computer-algebra system centered on finding symbolic solutions—for example, expressing an integral exactly instead of approximating it numerically. It searches substitutions, identities, rational decomposition and integration by parts, then differentiates and samples the result to check it. The same expression engine also supports differentiation, simplification, exact arithmetic, residue-theorem integration, vector calculus, and more.

Fun factThis project was built from the ground up, without using any symbolic math libraries. The core part was also written completely by hand.

Photon Primitives

A general framework for unbiased path connection, a vast family of geometric estimators, and a visual language for NPR. Complementary strategies and MIS turn that flexibility into measured rendering performance. Unpublished follow-ups explore surface footprints, signed derivatives, and the verified, fast photon hourglass implemented in RadianceLab.

Fun factOne photon sample can turn into a whole family of paths: release some of its coordinates and they sweep a curve, sheet or volume that a camera ray can hit.

Holography & depth reconstruction

Build off-axis holographic depth reconstruction inside a physically based path tracer, then expose the caveats hidden by the final depth map: illumination tilt and delay, sweep geometry, rolling shutters, wrapped phase, wavelength choice, discontinuities and multipath scattering. Interactive setups show where each distortion enters and how the reconstruction pipeline resolves it.

Fun factNo light waves interfere here: the 'hologram' is an algebraic analogy built from modulated illumination and sensor correlation.

Analytical Render · LTC

Graphics · Lighting

Linearly Transformed Cosines approximate a complex BRDF lobe with a transformed cosine, turning direct lighting from polygonal area lights—especially quads—into closed-form edge integrals. Compare that deterministic result against Monte Carlo in a material garage while the fit and geometry stay inspectable.

Fun factLet the Monte Carlo side converge and its noise disappears; what still differs is the LTC fit's bias, which no amount of sampling removes.

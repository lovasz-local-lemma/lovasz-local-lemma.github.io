BalanceForge / Control study

What is stopping
the controller?

A pendulum can be easy to hold and difficult to acquire. Separate the physical task, the information the network receives, and the search that learns its actions.

01 / STABILIZE

Stay up, even when pushed

Small-angle holding tests feedback and disturbance rejection. The goal allows twitching: every individual link must remain above horizontal.

02 / ACQUIRE

Build momentum, then catch

Swing-up must coordinate energy and phase while respecting the rail and acceleration limits. More successful holding does not by itself establish a successful swing-up.

03 / DIAGNOSE

Change one thing; measure again

Keep the policy fixed. Probe initial state, numerical resolution and actuation separately. Compare actual link motion with the fitness and the topology.

Physics rate, controller rate and screen frame rate

Most neural examples use 120 Hz physics. Terrain and learned Fourier arms use 60 Hz. The calibrated double/triple references integrate at 960 Hz but compute feedback at 60 Hz. Terrain evaluates its network at 60 Hz and holds actions at 30 Hz; the learned dodge scorer makes a new choice at 20 Hz. These are rates per simulation second, independent of the monitor refresh rate or Sim speed.

A temporary override changes only the display integrator. Neural sampling, planner cadence and forecast models retain their recorded timing. Per-step damping is adjusted to retain its decay per second. The selector turns amber and shows the original rate, saved catch-time estimates are suppressed, and another preset or training run clears the override. Physics choices must divide the unchanged controller interval into whole steps. Precomputed differential trajectories show their generating rate but have no live physics to override.

Each new experiment’s description reports its packaged replay measurements separately from the original audit. The NEAT 240 Hz result can change with roundoff-sized differences in how the starting angles are constructed; that sensitivity is part of this experiment.

The bound also scales the action

a = maxAccel × networkOutput. Doubling the bound doubles the same output’s drive. It changes feedback gain, not just spare capacity. Production velocity inputs also divide by an acceleration-dependent scale. Fixing that scale changes the outcome, but one promising CMA-ES local hold still fell from 19/20 to 0/20 under refinement.

Nudges can supply energy

The PPO momentum replay now enables tip impulses of 40 every 3–5 seconds after a two-second catch. Ten paired one-minute starts caught without rail contact with nudges either on or off. Matched larger knockdowns still escaped the actor’s reliable recovery region; random nudges did not reliably repair them. Toggle them off to isolate feedback.

Next experiment: keep observation units fixed, then train with a range of acceleration limits and recovery states. Measure when the actuator saturates, whether each push adds useful energy, and whether catch rate survives smaller timesteps. A failed frozen policy does not prove a state is physically unreachable. Energy and phase matter alongside actuation limits, as illustrated in MIT’s underactuated-control notes.

How long should I watch?

The ready controller strip shows measured simulation-time ranges. The PPO catch takes about 2.4 s, including a two-second confirmation within 60° of upright. Rigid-reference timings use their actual holding-phase test. A displayed catch allows motion; it is not a claim of motionless equilibrium. Manual pushes or edited parameters invalidate the undisturbed timing estimate.

A learned time estimator is practical with a sufficiently broad rollout dataset. It should predict a time distribution and the chance of never catching, keeping failed or unfinished runs as censored observations. For a small fixed catalog, measured ranges are easier to validate than another neural model. Neural time-to-event prediction

Case study / What information helps?

Predict motion, then learn which route to take

The older dodge CNN already sees the whole board. Its danger encoding, however, can alias bullets moving in different directions. The new controller supplies nine short physical forecasts and uses a CMA-ES-trained shared network to rank them. Forecasting is an explicit model; the 41 scoring parameters are learned. There is no fallback controller or knowledge of future bullets.

For each command, the forecast measures clearance over four time horizons, wall margin, centrality, velocity alignment and continuity with the previous command. It predicts currently visible bullets ballistically: b(t) = b(0) + vt. The learned score chooses a route; a high score is a preference, not a calibrated probability of survival.

A clean strike through a constrained course

The two ridges alter launch and landing angles; a movable bumper transfers momentum; the low beam limits high arcs. Beam contact is legal and produces a passive rebound. After the first real club impulse, the club freezes. The network cannot steer the airborne ball.

Would friction make swing-up easier?

Damping can help dissipate unwanted motion near a catch, but also removes energy during acquisition. The useful amount depends on the strategy. In this simulator, the damping control attenuates particle velocities; cart drag is a separate term. A fair ablation preserves damping per second when changing timestep.

Zero gravity is not a straightforward easier version of upright control. In our zero-command check, a static25° pose simply remained there: it already passed the above-horizontal criterion. A static140° pose remained below it. Neither result demonstrates an acquired balance strategy. Always compare against zero control before interpreting curriculum progress.

Compare online demos by their actual task

Force-driven examples do exist: DeepMind’s public cart-pole model uses a bounded force motor, and its two- and three-pole tasks start in swing-up configurations. Its ten-second default, observation, reward and plant differ from these90-second per-link tests. This portfolio’s evidence is the explicit interface, visible internals and reproducible limits; velocity control is not a universal explanation for other demonstrations. Interpretation / Six competing explanations

Make a diagnosis before adding training time

Open a hypothesis to see its signature and a discriminating experiment. Several limitations can coexist; a graph is evidence to investigate, not an automatic verdict.

01 · Information is missing or clipped

Look for: different angular velocities producing the same inputs, or channels flattening at their limits. Near horizontal, horizontal bob speed is a poor substitute for angular speed.

Test: train matched policies with signed rates or a history window. A frozen old network cannot consume a new encoding without adaptation. More hidden nodes cannot let a memoryless policy resolve two indistinguishable instantaneous inputs that require different actions.

02 · Actuation or rail bounds constrain the motion

Look for: sustained saturated commands, loss of rail margin and repeated impacts. An energy deficit can explain failure to reach a height; sufficient energy does not guarantee the right phase or a catch.

Test: compare a model-based trajectory under the same constraints; relax one physical bound as a counterfactual. Improvement suggests a constraint matters. Failure of one optimizer does not prove the constrained motion is impossible.

03 · The policy class is too restrictive

Look for: the teacher action changing sharply where a student’s output stays smooth, or persistent imitation error on informative, held-out inputs despite successful fitting by a larger policy. Topology size alone is not a measure of competence.

Test: fit a complete-state teacher using the same observation and action interface. Compare action error and closed-loop hold for linear, ReLU and recurrent policies. Successful imitation with a failed evolutionary search implicates discovery or objective design before representational impossibility.

04 · Fitness rewards the wrong achievement

Look for: rising fitness without longer physical holds; an upright-looking cart-to-tip direction while a middle rod falls; wall contacts helping the score.

Test: log every individual rod, rail contacts and final continuous hold beside reward. Keep shaped reward for learning, but promote examples using independent physical criteria. The live Control tab measures the displayed rollout, not population-wide success.

05 · Curriculum has no bridge to the next task

Look for: consistent performance at one stage followed by near-zero success after a small change, or forgetting earlier stages. Gravity-only progression can teach holding without ever teaching energy pumping.

Test: evaluate each checkpoint on its own stage, earlier stages and the next stage. Advance when there is repeatable overlap; retain some earlier cases. Reverse-start curricula sample states near a known goal state and expand outward. They require valid physical starts and still do not guarantee crossing an infeasible or disconnected region.

06 · Numerical resolution changes the task

Look for: sharp timestep sensitivity, large rod-length residuals, large frame-dependent changes, or high-frequency actions that exploit the integrator.

Test: double physics resolution while holding each command for its original duration and preserving damping per second. If the result disappears, do not promote it as robust control. Separate the simulator timestep, controller rate and observation quantization; they are different experiments.

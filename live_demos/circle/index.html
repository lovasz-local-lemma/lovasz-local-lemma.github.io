How round is round?

Draw-a-perfect-circle games turn a familiar shape into a numerical challenge. Yet a high score can accompany a visibly imperfect loop. That mismatch raises a geometric question: what should closeness to a circle actually mean?

This lab compares average error, worst-case deviation, compactness, local curvature, and harmonic structure. A stronger criterion should resist size tricks, expose local defects, and distinguish a complete loop from a well-fitted arc.

Inspect the counterexamples ↗

One local criterion

κ(s) = dθ/ds

A circle has constant curvature κ = 1/R. Measuring departures from that ideal reveals local defects, while differentiation also amplifies sampling noise.

What does gap completion change?

Straight: the shortest join adds no inferred shape. Its corners can affect curvature, especially across a wider gap.

Tangent-matched: a short cubic follows estimated endpoint directions. This softens the join, but does not match curvature or guarantee a better score. Unstable or crossing curves fall back to straight.

Only a nearly complete loop inside the release zone is joined. A short overshoot past the start can be trimmed; longer tails remain part of the stroke. The added seam is teal, discarded tail amber. Scores describe the completed contour; exports retain the captured stroke and all completion details.

What directional width leaves unresolved

Directional width sees only the convex hull, so outlines that share a hull have identical width traces and inward dent depth beyond that hull is not measured. A single outward speck can also control a support line. The slider stops below a = ⅛, where the minimum radius of curvature reaches zero and numerical curvature becomes singular.

Research basis: Hynd, support functions and constant width · Resnikoff, Fourier structure

06 The tangent walk Theorem-backed

Same total turning. Different local geometry.

A regular simple closed loop has one full tangent rotation. A circle distributes that turning uniformly along arclength.

Scale-space theory and this implementation

Curvature scale-space tracks how curvature features change under path-based Gaussian smoothing. This lab adds a nonstandard deviation heatmap and bending-energy view so convex shapes are not blank. Neither is an accepted metrology parameter, probability, or uncertainty interval.

Numerical limits and interpretation
  • Fine scales amplify pointer sampling and numerical differentiation.
  • Feature locations depend on the stroke start, and smoothing can merge or shift narrow dents.
  • Classical zero-crossing scale-space can be blank for both a circle and a noncircular convex shape.
  • Discrete curvature and bending estimates may rebound across scale because of aliasing.

Research basis: Mokhtarian & Mackworth (1986) · multiscale theory (1989)

Implementation and evidence

Explicit geometry makes the assumptions testable.

The geometry pipeline is deterministic: resampling, smoothing, fitting, and signal analysis have inspectable intermediate results. Known circles, transformed copies, local defects, and constant-width counterexamples distinguish numerical behavior from choices in the scoring policy.

Agreement with human preference remains a separate validation problem. The current calibration expresses a set of geometric priorities; it was not fitted to human ratings. That boundary matters when a plausible number conflicts with the visible shape—the observation that started this experiment.
